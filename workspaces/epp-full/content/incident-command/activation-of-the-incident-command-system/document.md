# Activation of the Incident Command System

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} activates the Incident Command System when an emergency, disaster, credible threat, or potential incident requires organized leadership and command-center coordination.

## Activation Triggers
Incident command may be activated when an event has occurred, is expected to occur, or has the potential to disrupt resident safety, staffing, facility systems, essential services, communications, evacuation, sheltering, or continuity of operations.

The person in charge of the facility at the time should assume the Incident Commander role or designate an appropriate Incident Commander until relieved through the facility's leadership process.

## Initial Activation
The Incident Commander or designee should notify facility leadership and command personnel using {{emergency_code_alert}} or another facility-approved alert phrase. The message should indicate that incident command is active, identify the incident or threat when appropriate, and direct activated leaders to report to or contact the command center.

The primary command center location is {{primary_command_center_location}}. If that location is unsafe, inaccessible, or unsuitable for the incident, command should use {{alternate_command_center_location}} or another location designated by the Incident Commander.

## Initial Command Period
At activation, the Incident Commander should gather enough information to understand the immediate threat, resident impact, staff impact, building conditions, communications status, and outside-agency involvement.

The Incident Commander should then:

- Decide which command positions or sections are needed.
- Assign immediate command roles and brief assigned leaders.
- Establish initial incident objectives.
- Identify urgent actions needed to protect residents, staff, visitors, and the facility.
- Confirm how command decisions and messages will be documented.
- Confirm which communication methods are functional.
- Determine whether hazard-specific procedures, evacuation procedures, sheltering procedures, lockdown, or other protective actions are needed.

Immediate objectives should be posted, shared, or otherwise made visible to activated command staff so that departments work from the same priorities.

## Job Action Sheets and Forms
Activated command staff should receive or access the job action sheets, forms, logs, and reference tools that apply to their assigned roles. These tools support role-specific action, but they remain separate documents for the later job-action-sheet and artifact pass.

Command should assign documentation responsibility early so that decisions, notifications, objectives, staff assignments, department reports, outside-agency direction, and major operational changes are captured during the response.

## Communication and External Coordination
The command center should confirm internal and external communication capability. Internal communication should reach resident-care areas, departments, labor pool staff, support teams, and other active response areas. External communication should reach emergency responders, emergency management, public health or regulatory contacts, receiving partners, vendors, transportation partners, or other organizations as needed for the incident.

If primary telephone, internet, paging, or radio systems are unavailable, the Incident Commander should approve backup communication methods and ensure staff understand which channel to use.

## Command Activation Boundaries
This document owns activation of the Section C Incident Command System. The all-hazards EOP activation document explains when the overall emergency program is activated. The evacuation activation document explains evacuation-specific activation steps. Organizational charts, job action sheets, command-center forms, and designated-area telephone charts are separate incident-command tools.

## What Staff Should Understand
- The person in charge begins or assigns incident command when an emergency requires organized leadership.
- Activation should identify the alert, command center, assigned leaders, initial objectives, and communication methods.
- The command structure should be scaled to the incident.
- Command decisions and notifications must be documented from the beginning.
- Job action sheets and forms support role-specific work but are maintained separately.
