# Concept of Operations

## Purpose
This document describes how {{facility_name}} uses the Incident Command System to organize emergency response, preserve resident services, maintain communication, and support continuity of operations during an incident.

## Incident Command Operating Model
The facility uses an incident command structure to provide clear leadership, role accountability, communication flow, documentation, and operational direction during emergencies. The structure is flexible and may be scaled to the size, risk, and duration of the event.

The Incident Commander is the only command role that must be active for every incident. The Incident Commander may activate additional command staff, Section Chiefs, unit leaders, or support roles when the incident requires more coordination than one person can safely manage.

## Incident Action Planning
Incident response should follow an incident action planning cycle. The cycle should be practical, documented, and repeated as the event changes.

The cycle includes:

- Assessing the situation and collecting current information.
- Establishing incident objectives.
- Selecting strategies to meet the objectives.
- Assigning tactical direction and needed resources.
- Monitoring whether assigned actions are working.
- Revising objectives and assignments when conditions change.

Incident objectives should be specific enough for staff to understand what must be done, who owns the action, what information must be reported back, and when the next status update is expected.

## Situation Assessment
The command center should collect situational information from internal and external sources. Internal information may include resident census, resident acuity, staff availability, building condition, utility status, supply status, security concerns, and the facility's ability to maintain essential services. External information may include emergency management updates, public health notices, weather or hazard information, response-agency guidance, partner reports, and other community situational intelligence.

When applicable, the facility may use {{emergency_reporting_system_name}} or another jurisdiction-approved reporting or information-sharing system. Department Rapid Assessment Forms and similar tools should feed current operational information to the command center, but the forms themselves remain separate artifacts.

## Command Team Coordination
Activated command roles should coordinate through briefings, status updates, assigned objectives, and documented communication. The Incident Commander should ensure that Operations, Planning, Logistics, Safety/Security/Liaison, Public Information, Finance, Documentation, and other roles are activated only as needed and understand their assigned scope.

Command should avoid duplicated work by assigning clear ownership for resident care, facility systems, staffing, transportation, supplies, outside coordination, public information, documentation, and cost tracking.

## Continuity of Operations
Incident command should support continuity of resident care and essential services. Command decisions should account for current resident needs, staffing capacity, service limitations, delegations of authority, succession needs, and whether the facility can continue to shelter, partially operate, evacuate, or transition into recovery.

When the incident disrupts normal leadership or operations, the Incident Commander should confirm who has authority to make time-sensitive decisions and how that authority will transition as conditions change.

## Training and Readiness
Staff who may serve in incident command roles should be trained on the facility's command structure, communication expectations, forms or logs used during response, and the relationship between command roles and hazard-specific procedures. Exercises and after-action reviews should evaluate whether command activation, communication flow, objectives, documentation, and role assignments worked as intended.

## Boundaries
This document owns the operating concept for incident command. Activation steps, organizational charts, role-specific job action sheets, forms, and command-center location charts are maintained in separate incident-command documents or tools.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Incident command creates a shared structure for leadership, communication, assignments, and documentation.
- The Incident Commander may scale the structure up or down based on incident needs.
- Incident objectives should be clear, documented, assigned, and revisited.
- Situation assessment should include resident status, staff status, building conditions, external intelligence, and continuity-of-operations risks.
- Detailed role duties and forms live in the job action sheets and tool artifacts.
