
# Staffing Recovery

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} restores safe staffing patterns and staff support after an emergency or disaster.

## Staffing Assessment
Leadership should assess current staffing by department, shift coverage, staff fatigue, staff injuries, transportation barriers, call-off patterns, sheltering needs, payroll or timekeeping issues, credentialed support, and services that cannot resume without additional staff.

## Recovery Priorities
Staffing recovery should prioritize resident care, life safety, medication administration, food service, environmental services, maintenance, communications, security, documentation, and supervision. Leadership should determine when emergency assignments can end, when normal schedules can resume, and whether temporary staffing support is still needed.

## Staff Support
Recovery may require rest periods, meal support, lodging coordination, transportation assistance, emotional support, critical incident stress resources, and clear communication about assignments and schedule changes. Staff should be released from extended duty only when resident-care coverage remains safe.

## Documentation and Follow-Up
Documentation should include staffing levels, critical gaps, emergency assignments, relief plans, staff support actions, labor costs, credentialing or volunteer use, and unresolved staffing risks.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Safe staffing remains a recovery priority until normal operations are stable.
- Report fatigue, transportation barriers, injuries, and assignment concerns.
- Do not leave assigned duty until properly relieved.
- Track emergency staffing decisions and support needs.
