
# Recovery Plan Overview

## Purpose
This overview describes how {{facility_name}} transitions from emergency response into immediate recovery, temporary recovery, and full recovery after a disaster or emergency incident.

## Recovery Stages
Immediate recovery includes actions taken to stabilize resident care, life safety, staffing, utilities, communications, supplies, and building operations until outside services or normal operations can resume.

Temporary recovery includes interim actions and equipment needed to operate safely when some systems, areas, staffing patterns, vendors, or services are not yet back to normal.

Full recovery is the return of the facility, services, systems, and documentation to normal operating conditions or to an approved alternate operating state.

## Command and Coordination
The command center or assigned leadership should manage recovery using the incident command structure until recovery responsibilities are reassigned or demobilized. Departments should report operational status, resource needs, resident-care impacts, staffing concerns, and damage information to the command center.

## Decision-Making
Leadership should use department rapid assessments, facility system status reports, resident-care needs, utility status, staffing levels, regulatory direction, and community conditions to decide whether to continue operations, relocate residents, evacuate, reopen areas, or request outside assistance.

## Documentation and Review
Recovery documentation should include assessments, decisions, costs, communications, resident impacts, repairs, supply replacement, staff support, and after-action findings.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Recovery begins while response actions may still be underway.
- Report department status and unmet needs promptly.
- Do not resume use of damaged areas or systems until cleared.
- Document recovery decisions and actions.
