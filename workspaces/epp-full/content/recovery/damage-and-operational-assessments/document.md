
# Damage and Operational Assessments

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} assesses damage, operational capability, and resident-care impact during recovery.

## Assessment Priorities
Assessment should begin when it is safe and should prioritize life safety, structural stability, utilities, fire protection, resident care, infection prevention, medications, food and water, communications, information systems, supplies, staffing, and access routes.

Maintenance and qualified support resources should evaluate building structure, utilities, equipment, hazardous materials, fire protection, HVAC, power, water, sewer, medical gases, and other facility systems. Department leaders should assess their areas and report operational capability to the command center.

## Department Reporting
Department assessments should identify whether the department is fully operational, limited, or non-operational; what services are affected; what resources are needed; what resident-care risks exist; and how long current staffing and supplies can support operations.

## Use of Findings
Incident command should use assessment findings to determine whether the facility can sustain operations, needs partial relocation, should reduce census, must evacuate, or requires outside support.

## Recovery Follow-Up
Assessment findings should be updated as conditions change. Documentation should support regulatory reporting, insurance, vendor work, cost tracking, after-action review, and plan revision.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Assess only when it is safe to do so.
- Report damage, unsafe areas, and service limits quickly.
- Include resident-care impact in every department report.
- Update assessments when conditions change.
