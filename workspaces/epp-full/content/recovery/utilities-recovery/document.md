
# Utilities Recovery

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} restores and verifies utility systems after an emergency or disaster.

## Utility Systems
Utilities recovery may include electrical service, emergency power, generator fuel, water, sewer, natural gas or propane, HVAC, steam, medical gases, vacuum, sump pumps, elevators, fire protection, and other systems that support resident care and building operations.

## Initial Recovery Actions
Maintenance or assigned leadership should confirm which systems are affected, what areas are served, what resident-care functions depend on them, what temporary workarounds are in place, and what vendor or public utility support is needed.

Systems should not be returned to normal use until appropriate inspection, testing, repair, and clearance have occurred. When partial service is restored, leadership should define limitations and communicate workarounds to affected departments.

## Coordination and Documentation
Incident command should prioritize utilities needed for life safety, resident care, food service, sanitation, communications, temperature control, medication storage, and equipment operation. Documentation should include outage duration, repairs, vendor contacts, generator/fuel status, test results, costs, and unresolved risks.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report utility problems and resident-care impacts immediately.
- Continue approved workarounds until normal service is cleared.
- Do not use equipment or areas affected by unsafe utilities.
- Document restoration times, limitations, and follow-up needs.
