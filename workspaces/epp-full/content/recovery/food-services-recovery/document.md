
# Food Services Recovery

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} restores food service operations after an emergency or disaster.

## Recovery Priorities
Food services recovery should prioritize safe food storage, resident meals, therapeutic diets, hydration, sanitation, refrigeration, food preparation equipment, staffing, vendor deliveries, and alternate meal plans if normal operations are not available.

## Assessment
Food service leadership should assess available food, water, equipment, refrigeration, freezer status, cooking capability, dishwashing, sanitation supplies, staffing, delivery access, and diet information. Any food exposed to unsafe temperatures, contamination, water damage, smoke, pests, or questionable storage conditions should be evaluated before use.

## Temporary Operations
If normal food preparation is limited, leadership should coordinate with nursing, incident command, vendors, and alternate suppliers to implement emergency menus, modified delivery, disposable service items, manual diet tracking, or alternate preparation methods.

## Return to Normal
Before resuming normal operations, food service should confirm that equipment, utilities, storage, sanitation, inventory, vendor supply, and staffing are adequate. Documentation should include discarded food, replacement supplies, vendor contacts, menu modifications, resident impacts, costs, and after-action findings.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Resident nutrition and hydration remain essential recovery priorities.
- Do not use food or equipment until safety is confirmed.
- Report diet, refrigeration, sanitation, and supply concerns quickly.
- Document menu changes and resident-care impacts.
