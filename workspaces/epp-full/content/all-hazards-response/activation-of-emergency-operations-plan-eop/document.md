# Activation of Emergency Operations Plan (EOP)

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} activates the Emergency Operations Plan when an emergency, disaster, serious disruption, or credible threat requires an organized all-hazards response.

## Activation Triggers
The Emergency Operations Plan may be activated when normal department procedures are not enough to protect residents, staff, visitors, essential services, or facility operations. Activation may be needed for facility-based events, community-based events, utility disruptions, fire or life-safety threats, security incidents, severe weather, supply or staffing disruption, evacuation threats, shelter-in-place conditions, or other hazards that require coordinated leadership.

Activation may begin with a notification, emergency code announcement, leadership assessment, command center activation, or immediate protective action. The person activating the plan should consider the seriousness of the event, expected duration, resident impact, staffing capacity, communications needs, outside-agency coordination, and whether the incident may escalate.

## Immediate Activation Steps
When immediate activation is needed, leadership should notify staff using the facility's all-hazards alert process. The default activation language is {{emergency_code_alert}}. The facility should review whether the sample general disaster code word remains appropriate: {{code_alert_has_been_provided_sample}}.

If the incident creates an immediate threat to life or safety, staff should begin the preplanned protective actions that apply to the hazard. When the facility uses a separate term for a fire situation, the term should be maintained here: {{code_word_term_identify_fire_situation}}.

After the activation notice is issued, designated leadership staff should report to or contact the command center, while other staff should return to assigned areas for instructions unless the incident-specific procedure directs a different action.

## Notifications and Coordination
The activating leader or Incident Commander should make or delegate notifications based on the type and severity of the event. Notifications may include internal leadership, department heads, direct-care staff, residents or representatives when appropriate, emergency response agencies, emergency management, regulatory contacts, vendors, receiving partners, or other support organizations.

Notification responsibilities should use current position titles, contact methods, and escalation paths. The facility-specific notification owner or position title is {{training_program_owner_title}}. External notification references should include {{regulatory_agency_name}} and {{name_county_emergency_management_agency}} when those contacts apply to the incident.

Throughout the emergency preparedness program, the facility should use its approved executive-leadership terminology consistently. If the facility uses Executive Director or another title instead of Administrator, apply the approved terminology decision here: {{using_executive_director_change_throughout_plan}}.

## Transition to Incident Command
Activation of the Emergency Operations Plan starts the organized response. Depending on the event, leadership may activate the command center, assign an Incident Commander, activate incident command roles, begin documentation, request department status reports, or open an incident action planning cycle.

This document owns the all-hazards EOP activation trigger and first-response expectations. The all-hazards Activation of the Incident Command System document describes command center activation steps, and the Section C Incident Command System documents contain the deeper incident command structure, job action sheets, forms, and role-specific guidance.

## What Staff Should Understand
- EOP activation means the facility is moving into a coordinated emergency response.
- Staff should listen for the approved activation code or plain-language alert and follow assigned-area instructions.
- Immediate protective actions may begin before all leadership notifications are complete.
- The activating leader or Incident Commander may assign command roles, request status reports, and coordinate outside notifications.
- Detailed incident command role duties live in the incident command documents, not in this activation overview.
