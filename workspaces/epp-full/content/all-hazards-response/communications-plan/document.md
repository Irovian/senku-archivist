# Communications Plan

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} maintains an all-hazards communication plan for emergency response, including contact readiness, internal and external communication methods, information sharing, and coordination with related communication procedures.

## Communication Framework
The communication plan supports timely communication with staff, physicians, emergency response agencies, emergency management, residents, resident representatives, other healthcare facilities, vendors, volunteers, and other contacts needed during emergency operations.

The plan should be reviewed with the emergency preparedness program and updated when contact information, communication methods, reporting systems, leadership roles, technology, or external partner relationships change.

The facility's primary emergency contact is {{primary_emergency_contact_name}}. State long-term care ombudsman contact information is maintained as {{state_ltc_ombudsman_contact}}.

## Contact Readiness
The facility should maintain current contact information for staff, physicians, emergency management contacts, state licensing or certification contacts, resident representatives, receiving facilities, vendors, transportation resources, and other support organizations. Contact lists should be accessible to leaders who may activate the Emergency Operations Plan or Incident Command System.

Contact information should be protected from becoming outdated. Updates should be made when staff roles change, agencies change reporting expectations, vendors change after-hours contacts, or emergency communication systems are added or retired.

## Communication Methods
The communication plan should identify primary and alternate methods that may be used during an emergency. These may include normal telephones, intercom or public address systems, radios, cell phones, pagers, secure messaging systems, emergency reporting systems, websites, hotlines, runners, written notices, or other approved methods.

The Internal Communications During a Disaster document owns staff-facing and command-center communication methods inside the facility. The External Communications During a Disaster document owns communication with outside agencies, partners, public-information channels, and community-facing methods.

## Information Sharing During Response
During an emergency, communication should support accurate status reporting, resident safety, continuity of care, resource requests, and coordination with response partners. Information shared externally should follow applicable privacy, security, and authorization expectations.

The Incident Commander or designee should determine what information must be shared, who is authorized to share it, which method should be used, and how communication should be documented. Resident-specific information, condition updates, occupancy reports, resource needs, and facility status should be coordinated through the related communication, resident medical information, evacuation, and recovery procedures.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic should be read with:

- Emergency Codes, which defines staff alert language and alert methods.
- Activation of Emergency Operations Plan (EOP), which explains initial activation notification.
- Internal Communications During a Disaster, which covers staff and facility communication methods.
- External Communications During a Disaster, which covers agencies, partners, public information, and outside communication channels.
- Resident and Family Information, which covers sharing emergency plan information with residents and representatives.
- Resident Medical Information and evacuation documents, which cover resident-specific information transfer during emergency response.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Emergency communication depends on current contact information and usable primary and alternate methods.
- Internal and external communication duties are related but not the same.
- Only authorized staff should release facility, resident, or public information during an emergency.
- Communication methods may change if phones, power, internet, or other systems are disrupted.
- Detailed internal and external communication procedures live in their own all-hazards documents.
