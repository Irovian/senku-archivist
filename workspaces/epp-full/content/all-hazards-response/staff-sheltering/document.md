# Staff Sheltering

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} supports staff who must remain on site, rest between assignments, or shelter at or near the facility during extended emergency operations.

## Activation
The command center or assigned leader should activate staff sheltering when staff cannot safely leave, relief staff cannot report, weather or security conditions restrict travel, evacuation or shelter-in-place extends operations, or the facility needs staff to remain available for resident care continuity.

Staff sheltering should be coordinated through {{staffing_resource_owner_title}} or the current staffing lead. Staff sheltering does not replace resident sheltering, family sheltering, pet sheltering, or public shelter operations.

## Shelter Locations and Setup
Pre-designated staff sheltering or rest locations include {{staff_sheltering_locations}}. Setup responsibility is assigned to {{staff_sheltering_setup_owner_title}} or the current department assigned by the command center.

Staff sheltering areas should support rest, privacy where feasible, hygiene, charging of essential devices when available, communication with the command center, and safe separation from resident care areas unless operationally necessary.

Alternate staff sheltering or lodging options include {{staff_sheltering_alternate_locations}}. Partner agreements, lodging arrangements, shelter contacts, or informal support options are described by {{staff_sheltering_partner_agreements}}.

## Operations During Staff Sheltering
The staffing lead should track which staff are remaining on site, who needs rest space, who is scheduled for the next shift, and who may need meals, hydration, medications, transportation support, or emotional support.

Staff sheltering should be coordinated with food service, environmental services, security, utilities, and communications. Staff should receive clear instructions on where to rest, how to be reached, when to return to duty, and which areas remain off limits.

Staff family sheltering and staff pet sheltering require separate leadership approval and should follow the specific procedures for those topics when activated.

## Recovery
When staff sheltering is no longer needed, the command center should release sheltering areas, arrange cleaning and reset, reconcile staff assignment records, identify staff support needs, and capture improvement items for after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Staff sheltering supports emergency staffing continuity during extended operations.
- Staff should use only assigned sheltering or rest areas.
- Staff must remain reachable for assignment updates while resting or sheltering.
- Staff family and pet sheltering are separate decisions and separate procedures.
- Report fatigue, medication needs, transportation barriers, or safety concerns to the staffing lead.
