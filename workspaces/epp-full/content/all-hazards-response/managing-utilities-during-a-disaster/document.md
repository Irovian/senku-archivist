# Managing Utilities During a Disaster

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} monitors, prioritizes, conserves, and restores critical utility services during a disaster or utility disruption.

## Utility Management Framework
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} is responsible for coordinating utility assessment and facility support actions unless the Incident Commander, command center leader, or designee assigns another responsible role. Utility management should focus first on resident safety, life-sustaining systems, fire and life safety systems, infection prevention, sanitation, food and water support, medication and clinical operations, and safe building conditions.

The facility plans utility support around a target sustainment period of {{resource_sustainment_duration}}, while recognizing that actual capability depends on the hazard, equipment status, staffing, fuel, vendors, municipal services, and other incident conditions.

Departments should report utility problems, abnormal equipment conditions, conservation needs, and service limitations to the command center or assigned leader. Utility decisions should be documented when they affect resident care, essential operations, fire and life safety systems, emergency power, water, sewer, heating, cooling, elevators, medical equipment, food service, laundry, or waste handling.

## Emergency Power and Fuel
Emergency power planning should identify the fuel source, available runtime, load-shedding capability, supported services, and services that cannot continue when the facility is operating on emergency power. The generator fuel type is {{generator_fuel_type}}. The estimated full-load runtime before refueling is {{generator_full_load_runtime}}. If load can be shed safely, the estimated extended runtime is {{generator_load_shed_runtime}}.

Services supported by emergency power include {{emergency_power_supported_services}}. Services that cannot be provided or may be limited while on emergency power include {{emergency_power_unsupported_services}}. Areas and critical equipment supported by emergency power include {{emergency_power_supported_areas_equipment}}.

Detailed generator equipment data, fuel vendors, portable generator connection information, and fuel-source tables belong in the related resource/list documents. Loss of Emergency Generator Power owns response actions when the generator or emergency power system fails.

## Utility Service Impacts
Utility disruptions may affect heating, cooling, lighting, elevators, potable water, non-potable water, sewer or waste systems, natural gas or propane, steam pressure, communications infrastructure, fire protection systems, kitchen operations, laundry, clinical equipment, and other facility systems. The facility-specific utility services most likely to be affected are {{utility_impacted_services}}.

When a utility service is lost or compromised, responsible departments should assess:

- whether resident care or life safety is immediately affected;
- whether the condition requires emergency power, conservation, shutdown, relocation, evacuation, or vendor support;
- whether a regulatory report, municipal notification, fire watch, or other external coordination is needed;
- whether related procedures, such as loss of generator power, loss of water service, loss of sewer or waste system, or loss of fire protection, should be activated.

## Conservation and Continuity
The command center or assigned leader should direct conservation actions when utility capacity is limited. Conservation may include reducing nonessential electrical loads, limiting water use, modifying dining and laundry operations, adjusting heating or cooling priorities, moving residents away from unsafe areas, using alternate equipment, and delaying nonessential activities.

Utility decisions should align with the resource-management overview, the critical-supplies responsibility matrix, the 96-hour capability baseline, and the applicable hazard-specific utility procedures.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Utility problems must be reported early because small failures can affect resident care quickly.
- Emergency power does not mean every service in the building can operate normally.
- Conservation actions should be directed through the command center or assigned leader.
- Detailed quantities, fuel vendors, and equipment specifications belong in resource/list documents, not this overview.
