# Managing Resources and Assets

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} manages critical resources and assets during a disaster so resident care, staff support, facility operations, and emergency response activities can continue when normal supply channels are disrupted.

## Resource Management Priorities
Resource management should support resident safety, continuity of essential services, protection of staff, conservation of limited supplies, timely replenishment, and documentation of resource decisions. Resources and assets may include supplies, equipment, utilities, facility space, staff support resources, vendor services, transportation support, and other items needed to maintain operations.

The facility plans to sustain critical resources for {{resource_sustainment_duration}} unless the hazard vulnerability analysis, state or local requirements, or incident conditions require a different planning assumption. The basis for the sustainment target is {{resource_sustainment_basis}}.

## Resource Assessment
 The Incident Commander, command center leader, or designee should direct departments to assess current resources, expected burn rate, operational constraints, and remaining capability. Departments should use the Department Rapid Assessment Form or equivalent assessment process located at {{department_rapid_assessment_form_location}}.

Assessment should consider:

- current inventory on hand;
- expected resident census and acuity;
- staffing levels needed to maintain essential services;
- damaged, unavailable, or inaccessible supplies and equipment;
- vendor, transportation, utility, and communication constraints;
- whether conservation, substitution, sharing, or escalation is needed.

Assessment results should be reported through the command center or assigned leadership structure so resource decisions can be prioritized and documented.

## Resource Sources and Replenishment
Normal vendors and supply channels should be used when available. When normal sources are unavailable, delayed, or insufficient, the facility should use contingency sources such as {{resource_vendor_partner_sources}}.

Departments assigned to critical resources should monitor inventory, reorder before supplies reach critical levels, and notify the command center when supply availability may affect resident care or facility operations. Replenishment decisions should consider regional impacts, delivery reliability, mutual aid or partner availability, and whether the facility may need to support or receive support from other healthcare organizations.

## Stockpiling and Conservation
When a foreseeable event may interrupt access to supplies or services, the command center or assigned leader should direct departments to build inventories before access is lost, when doing so is safe and feasible. Stockpiling should focus first on supplies and services required for resident care, food and water, medications, medical supplies, sanitation, waste handling, emergency power support, staff support, and other critical operations.

If the situation does not allow inventory buildup or supplies become limited, departments should implement conservation strategies. Conservation may include reducing nonessential activities, substituting available supplies, adjusting service frequency when clinically appropriate, consolidating operations, prioritizing critical equipment, and escalating shortages that may affect resident safety.

Conservation plans and emergency resource lists should be communicated through {{conservation_plan_communication_methods}} and other methods approved by the Incident Commander, command center leader, or designee.

## Recovery and Documentation
Resource decisions should be documented when they affect resident care, supply levels, staffing, vendors, emergency purchases, conservation actions, resource sharing, or service limitations. Documentation may include department assessments, inventory records, purchase records, command center logs, situation reports, action plans, and recovery records.

During recovery, departments should replenish depleted or damaged items, reconcile borrowed or shared resources, capture costs, identify supply-chain gaps, and recommend updates to resource lists, vendor arrangements, conservation plans, and related emergency procedures.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Resource management begins with current assessment, not assumptions from the baseline plan alone.
- Critical resources may need to be conserved, substituted, replenished, or obtained from alternate sources.
- The command center or assigned leadership structure prioritizes resource decisions during the response.
- Staff should report shortages early and document resource actions that affect resident care or operations.
- Client facilities may solve the same operational constraint in different ways, so resource procedures must preserve configurable options.
