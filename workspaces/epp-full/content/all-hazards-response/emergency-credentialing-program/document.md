# Emergency Credentialing Program

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} may grant and manage temporary disaster privileges for licensed practitioners or qualified personnel who support resident care during emergency operations.

## Activation
Emergency credentialing may be used when the Emergency Operations Plan is activated and the facility cannot meet resident needs with existing staff, receives residents from another facility with accompanying personnel, or is asked to support an influx of residents or personnel during a declared or operational emergency.

The Incident Commander, Administrator, Medical Director or designee, Director of Nursing or designee, and other assigned leaders should coordinate the decision to use emergency credentialing.

## Identity and Qualification Review
Before a person provides resident care under disaster privileges, the facility should confirm identity and qualifications to the extent feasible under emergency conditions. Acceptable evidence may include government photo identification, current professional license or certification, organizational identification, disaster-response team identification, authority granted by a government entity, or confirmation by a current staff member with personal knowledge of the individual.

Primary source verification should be completed when feasible and as soon as the immediate emergency allows. The facility should follow applicable law, licensing requirements, medical staff policy, nursing policy, and emergency credentialing rules.

## Assignment and Supervision
Individuals granted disaster privileges should receive a clear assignment, reporting structure, scope of permitted activity, identification method, and supervision expectations before beginning work.

Department managers or assigned supervisors should observe the work of personnel with disaster privileges and make sure documentation, clinical record review, medication administration, infection prevention, privacy, and resident-safety expectations are followed.

Disaster privileges should be limited to the emergency need and should not be treated as routine employment, routine contracting, or permanent credentialing.

## Documentation and Reassessment
The facility should document the individual granted disaster privileges, evidence reviewed, assignment, supervisor, start time, scope of work, and any limits or concerns.

As soon as the immediate situation is under control, and preferably within 72 hours, leadership should determine whether disaster privileges need to continue. Disaster privileges should be ended when the emergency need has passed, verification cannot be completed, performance concerns arise, or the facility returns to normal staffing and credentialing processes.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Emergency credentialing is used only when emergency operations require additional qualified support.
- Identity and qualifications must be reviewed to the extent feasible before resident care is provided.
- Individuals with disaster privileges must have assigned supervision and clear work limits.
- Disaster privileges are temporary and must be reassessed as conditions stabilize.
- Normal credentialing, privacy, documentation, infection prevention, and resident-safety expectations still apply.
