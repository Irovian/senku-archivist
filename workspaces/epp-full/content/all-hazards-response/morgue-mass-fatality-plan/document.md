# Morgue / Mass Fatality Plan

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} manages deceased residents and mass-fatality conditions when an incident exceeds normal morgue, funeral-home, or body-holding processes.

## Activation
The Incident Commander, Administrator, Director of Nursing, Medical Director or designee, command center, or assigned leader should consider this procedure when a disaster or incident results in multiple deaths, overwhelms normal storage capacity, delays funeral-home response, or requires coordination with authorities.

This procedure should be coordinated with clinical leadership, infection prevention, security, social services, pastoral care when available, communication procedures, and local or state requirements for death reporting and disposition.

## Immediate Coordination
Staff should follow facility policy for pronouncement, notification, documentation, and respectful handling of the deceased. The command center should identify the current number of deceased residents, expected capacity needs, available storage options, security needs, and required notifications.

The facility should contact appropriate authorities, funeral homes, emergency management partners, or other support resources when normal processes cannot meet the need.

## Identification and Personal Belongings
Each deceased resident should be identified and tracked using the facility's assigned process. Identification should remain with the body and should be reflected in facility documentation.

Personal belongings should be secured, labeled, documented, and kept with the body or in another controlled location according to facility policy and incident conditions.

## Temporary Holding and Security
If normal morgue or storage capacity is exceeded or likely to be exceeded, the Incident Commander or assigned leader should determine whether temporary holding areas can be established. Infection prevention and safety considerations should guide temporary location selection.

Temporary holding areas should be secure, respectful, access-controlled, protected from public view, and monitored for environmental and infection-control concerns. Staff should preserve emergency egress and avoid placing temporary holding areas where they disrupt resident care.

## Family, Resident, and Staff Support
Communication with family members or responsible parties should be coordinated through assigned leadership and should follow legal, clinical, and privacy requirements. Staff should make reasonable efforts to respect cultural, spiritual, and religious considerations within available resources and applicable authority direction.

Pastoral care, social services, behavioral health support, or other support resources may be requested for families, residents, and staff.

## Recovery
After normal processes resume, the command center should reconcile records, confirm disposition arrangements, secure remaining belongings, document costs or resource requests, identify staff and family support needs, and capture improvement items for after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Treat deceased residents with dignity, privacy, and careful identification.
- Follow assigned reporting, documentation, and notification procedures.
- Temporary morgue or holding areas require command approval and infection-prevention input.
- Security, access control, and privacy are essential during mass-fatality operations.
- Family, resident, and staff support needs should be escalated early.
