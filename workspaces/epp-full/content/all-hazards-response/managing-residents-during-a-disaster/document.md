# Managing Residents During a Disaster

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} protects residents, maintains essential care, and coordinates resident services during emergency operations.

## Activation
The command center, Incident Commander, Administrator, Director of Nursing, or assigned leader should activate resident-management actions when an incident may affect resident safety, care delivery, admissions, transfers, discharge, shelter-in-place, evacuation, surge, resident movement, or access to resident information.

Resident-management actions should be coordinated with the specific hazard procedure, incident command assignments, staffing procedures, communication procedures, medical-record procedures, and evacuation or surge plans when activated.

## Resident Assessment and Care
Nursing and assigned department leaders should assess resident needs and report conditions that may affect care continuity. Assessments should consider acuity, medications, treatments, dietary needs, hydration, mobility, cognition, behavior, isolation or infection control needs, oxygen or equipment dependence, physician orders, and resident preferences when known.

The command center should support care modifications when normal services are disrupted. This may include prioritizing essential services, modifying schedules, arranging additional monitoring, requesting supplies, adjusting activities, coordinating meals and hydration, or preparing for transfer, discharge, shelter-in-place, or evacuation.

Care changes should remain consistent with resident rights, clinical judgment, applicable orders, infection prevention expectations, and available emergency resources.

## Resident Information and Tracking
The facility should maintain access to information needed for resident care continuity. This may include medication administration records, treatment records, allergies, diagnoses, diet orders, responsible-party contacts, physician contacts, equipment needs, and current location.

If electronic systems are unavailable or unreliable, the command center should activate the facility's backup process for accessing, printing, copying, or distributing resident information. Resident movement, transfer, discharge, evacuation, or surge placement should be tracked using the applicable resident tracking process.

## Resident and Responsible Party Communication
Residents should receive information appropriate to the incident, their care needs, and their ability to understand and participate in decisions. Responsible parties should be updated through approved communication channels when resident condition, location, transfer, discharge, evacuation, or significant service changes require notification.

Public information and media messaging should remain coordinated through the assigned communication process.

## Coordination With Related Plans
This procedure should remain an all-hazards resident-care overview. Detailed instructions for full evacuation, resident placement, resident medical records, medications, resident personal belongings, census reduction, surge capacity, and specific service failures belong in their dedicated documents.

## Recovery
As the incident stabilizes, leaders should restore normal care routines, reconcile resident locations and records, confirm follow-up needs, document unresolved care concerns, communicate status updates, and capture improvement items for after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Resident safety and essential care remain the first priority during emergency operations.
- Resident needs may require modified services, alternate care processes, or movement to safer locations.
- Resident information and location tracking must stay current during transfers, evacuation, surge, or service disruption.
- Communication with residents and responsible parties should follow approved facility processes.
- Detailed evacuation, surge, census reduction, and medical-record procedures live in their dedicated documents.
