# Surge Capacity Plan

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} evaluates and manages a request to receive additional residents or support surge capacity during a declared disaster or emergency operations.

## Activation
The command center, Incident Commander, Administrator, Director of Nursing, or assigned leader should consider surge capacity actions when another facility is evacuating, an emergency management partner requests surge space, incoming residents arrive unexpectedly, or the facility is asked to accept residents beyond routine admissions.

Surge capacity decisions should consider resident safety, staffing, supplies, licensed capacity, available beds, infection prevention, utilities, oxygen or equipment needs, dietary needs, medical records, family communication, and regulatory or emergency management expectations.

## Initial Notification and Information Gathering
The person receiving a surge request should document the message and immediately notify the person in charge. When possible, the facility should collect:

- sending facility or requesting agency contact information;
- number of residents expected and estimated arrival time;
- resident acuity, gender mix, mobility needs, behavioral or wandering precautions, isolation or infection-control concerns, and equipment needs;
- whether medications, charts, staff, supplies, beds, mattresses, or equipment will accompany the residents;
- requested duration of surge support and any known transportation or staging needs.

## Capacity and Resource Assessment
Leadership should compare the request with current census, available beds, staffing, food and water support, medication and medical-supply availability, oxygen and equipment capacity, laundry and linen capacity, environmental services, maintenance support, emergency power limits, and resident-care workload.

The facility should use existing licensed beds first when appropriate. If surge beyond normal licensed capacity is being considered, leadership should coordinate with applicable authorities before implementation whenever feasible.

## Receiving and Triage
When accepting incoming residents, the command center should determine whether a triage or receiving area is needed. If established, the area should have appropriate staffing, supplies, infection-prevention controls, documentation support, food or hydration support, and a clear path for resident placement.

Staff should document incoming residents, review available records, collect minimum essential information if records are incomplete, identify medications and equipment received, notify physicians or responsible parties as needed, and establish interim care plans.

## Surge Areas and Continuing Care
Surge areas should be selected based on resident safety, staffing visibility, emergency power availability, toilet and sink access, infection prevention, egress, privacy, lighting, call-device availability, equipment needs, and proximity to care support.

Surge arrangements should not block exits, corridors, emergency response access, fire/life-safety systems, or essential operations. Resident care in surge areas should include clinical monitoring, medication management, hydration, nutrition, activities or psychosocial support when feasible, personal belongings control, and ongoing status reporting to the command center.

## Communication and Recovery
Communication with sending facilities, families or responsible parties, physicians, regulators, emergency management partners, and media should be coordinated through the assigned communication process.

When surge operations end, leadership should reconcile residents and equipment received, coordinate return or discharge arrangements, restore surge areas, document costs and resource use, identify unresolved resident or staff needs, and capture improvement items for after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Surge capacity requires leadership approval and careful assessment of resident safety, staffing, supplies, space, and regulatory expectations.
- Incoming resident information should be documented as completely as possible.
- Surge areas must preserve egress, life safety, infection prevention, and resident dignity.
- The facility should use normal licensed beds first when feasible.
- Detailed staffing, credentialing, medical records, and communication processes remain in their dedicated documents.
