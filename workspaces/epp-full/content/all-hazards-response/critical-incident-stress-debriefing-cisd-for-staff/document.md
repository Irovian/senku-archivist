# Critical Incident Stress Debriefing (CISD) for Staff

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} monitors staff well-being and arranges emotional or psychological support after a critical incident or disaster response.

## Activation
The command center, Incident Commander, Administrator, Human Resources, or assigned leader should consider staff support when an incident involves death, serious injury, threat of violence, extended operations, evacuation, resident surge, staff sheltering, unusual distress, or other conditions likely to affect staff well-being.

CISD or other support should be matched to the event and to staff needs. Support may include leader check-ins, defusing discussions, referral to an Employee Assistance Program, access to chaplaincy or social services support, referral to external behavioral health resources, or formal CISD facilitation when appropriate.

## Monitoring Staff Well-Being
Leaders should monitor staff for fatigue, distress, withdrawal, agitation, impaired concentration, conflict, repeated exposure to traumatic details, and other signs that additional support may be needed.

Staff should be encouraged to report their own support needs and concerns about co-workers. Requests for support should be handled respectfully and shared only with personnel who need the information to coordinate assistance.

## Arranging Support
Human Resources or the assigned leader should coordinate available staff-support resources through the command center. If outside support is requested, the facility should confirm the service being requested, expected timing, location, privacy needs, and any limits on the type of support available.

Participation in debriefing or support activities should not interfere with resident care coverage. Supervisors should coordinate release from duty, breaks, or follow-up support so essential services remain covered.

## Recovery
After the event, leadership should document staff-support actions at the process level, identify unresolved staff needs, coordinate follow-up resources, and include staff-support lessons learned in after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Staff well-being is monitored during and after emergency operations.
- Staff may request emotional support or report concerns through supervisors or the assigned support process.
- CISD, EAP, chaplaincy, social services, or outside support may be used depending on the incident.
- Staff-support discussions should be handled respectfully and with appropriate privacy.
- Resident care coverage must be maintained while staff support is arranged.
