# Introduction

## Purpose
This document introduces the full building evacuation section for {{facility_name}} and explains when the evacuation plan is used.

## Evacuation Planning Scope
The evacuation plan supports partial or full movement of residents, staff, visitors, records, medications, supplies, and essential equipment when remaining in the affected area is unsafe or no longer operationally practical. Evacuation may be urgent, emergent, or planned, depending on the hazard, available time, resident acuity, building conditions, transportation access, and direction from emergency responders.

This section focuses on full building evacuation and vertical or external movement. Unit-level horizontal movement, immediate fire response, shelter-in-place decisions, and incident-specific protective actions are addressed in the related emergency procedures.

## Command Decision
The Incident Commander or designee should decide whether to begin evacuation in consultation with emergency services when available. The decision should consider life safety, the condition of the building, utility status, staffing, resident needs, weather or community conditions, transportation availability, and whether outside assistance can reach the facility.

When an external temporary stop-over point is needed, the facility should use {{temporary_stopover_location}} or another location approved by incident command for the event. Stop-over use should remain temporary until residents can safely return, transfer, discharge, or move to receiving partners.

## Coordination With Receiving Partners
The facility should maintain evacuation relationships, mutual aid arrangements, or receiving options appropriate to the resident population. Receiving partner information is maintained through {{receiving_facility_partners}} and the related receiving-facility resource documents.

## Related Tools and Deferred Artifacts
The evacuation algorithm, evacuation tracking form, evacuation floor plans, receiving-facility list, resource lists, and appendix materials are maintained as separate tools. This introduction establishes the section purpose and decision framework without duplicating those artifacts.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Evacuation may be partial, full, urgent, emergent, or planned.
- Incident command coordinates building-wide evacuation decisions.
- The stop-over point is temporary and may change based on conditions.
- Resident tracking, floor plans, receiving-facility lists, and forms are separate tools that support this plan.
