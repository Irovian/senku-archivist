# Evacuation Order

## Purpose
This document describes how {{facility_name}} issues and manages the order to evacuate specific areas or the full building.

## Issuing the Order
The Incident Commander should issue the evacuation order after considering the hazard, resident risk, building conditions, available staff, holding area readiness, transportation status, and emergency-services direction when available. If immediate danger requires action before command is fully established, the person in charge of the affected area may begin life-safety movement and notify command as soon as possible.

The order should identify the area to evacuate, route or vertical movement method, assigned holding area or discharge point, expected timing, and any safety limits. If elevators are cleared for use, staff should follow the approved elevator plan for {{elevator_locations}}. If elevators are unavailable or unsafe, staff should use the approved stairwell, evacuation device, or alternate vertical transport process, including {{alternate_vertical_transport_methods}} where applicable.

## Dispatching Evacuation Groups
The labor pool or command center should dispatch the needed evacuation groups to the affected area. The resident-care leader for the area should confirm when groups arrive and should not begin non-emergent movement until residents can be safely handed off.

As each area evacuates, staff should communicate status to the command center, report barriers, and confirm when the area has been cleared. The facility should use its approved room-clearing marker or equivalent process to show which rooms have been checked.

## Handoff and Tracking
Residents should be moved with identification, needed records, medications, essential belongings, and tracking documentation as required by the related resident preparation and tracking tools. The command center should confirm that holding areas have enough capacity before directing additional areas to evacuate.

## What Staff Should Understand
- The evacuation order should identify what area moves, where it moves, and which route is used.
- Non-emergent resident movement should wait until evacuation support is in place.
- Cleared rooms and completed areas must be communicated to command.
- Resident tracking and handoff documentation are required throughout movement.
