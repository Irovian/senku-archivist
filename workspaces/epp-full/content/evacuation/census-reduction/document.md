# Census Reduction

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} may reduce census before or during evacuation when safe discharge, transfer, or relocation can reduce risk and preserve care capacity.

## When Census Reduction May Be Used
Census reduction may be considered when the facility has warning before a hazard, when evacuation is likely but not yet immediate, when staffing or utility conditions threaten sustained operations, or when residents can safely leave with responsible parties or transfer to another appropriate setting.

Census reduction is not a substitute for resident assessment or discharge planning. Each decision should reflect resident condition, care needs, decision-making capacity, responsible-party involvement, transportation, medication access, medical records, follow-up needs, and receiving location suitability.

## Review and Approval
The Operations Section Chief, resident-care leadership, social services, nursing leadership, or other assigned leaders should identify residents who may be appropriate for discharge, transfer, or temporary relocation. Decisions should be approved through the command center or the facility's normal discharge and transfer authority when time allows.

Residents should not be released unless the facility can support a safe handoff, provide necessary information, and document where the resident is going.

## Communication and Documentation
The facility should document the resident's destination, responsible party or receiving contact, transportation method, needed records, medications, equipment, and any follow-up instructions. Responsible parties should receive clear instructions on care needs, return process, and who to contact for updates.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Census reduction is used only when it can be done safely.
- Resident condition and destination suitability drive the decision.
- Residents released to families or other locations still need documentation and tracking.
- Command should know which residents have left and where they went.
