# Resident Placement Into Other Facilities

## Purpose
This document describes how {{facility_name}} coordinates resident placement into other facilities during evacuation.

## Placement Decision
The Incident Commander should coordinate placement decisions with Operations, Planning, resident-care leadership, receiving partners, and emergency services when applicable. Placement should account for resident acuity, service needs, isolation or infection-control needs, memory-care needs, equipment, medications, payer or administrative constraints when time allows, transportation type, distance, and receiving-facility capacity.

The facility should first consider prearranged partners, mutual aid relationships, and appropriate nearby healthcare or residential settings. Receiving options are maintained through {{receiving_facility_partners}} and the separate receiving-facility resource documents.

## Stop-Over Use
When immediate transfer to a receiving facility is not possible, residents may move temporarily to {{temporary_stopover_location}} or another command-approved stop-over point. A stop-over point should be treated as temporary staging, not as a replacement for appropriate receiving placement.

## Handoff Information
Before a resident leaves, staff should send or make available enough information to support continuity of care. This may include resident identification, diagnosis or care summary, medications, allergies, code status, advance directives, responsible-party information, physician or provider information, special precautions, equipment, and destination.

## Resident and Family Communication
Responsible parties should be updated using the facility communication process as soon as practical. Updates should identify the resident's destination or expected destination when known and should protect resident privacy.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Receiving placement must match resident needs and available capacity.
- Stop-over locations are temporary.
- Residents should leave with information needed for continuity of care.
- Responsible parties should be informed through the approved communication process.
