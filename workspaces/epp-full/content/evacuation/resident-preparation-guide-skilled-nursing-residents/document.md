# Resident Preparation Guide - Skilled Nursing Residents

## Purpose
This guide describes how {{facility_name}} prepares Skilled Nursing residents for evacuation.

## Clinical Preparation
Skilled Nursing residents should be assessed for acuity, mobility, transfer method, oxygen or suction needs, medication timing, wound care, isolation status, dialysis or specialty-service needs, diet or swallowing precautions, cognitive status, behavior risk, fall risk, and whether clinical staff must accompany the resident.

Nursing leadership should identify high-risk residents early and coordinate with command on evacuation priority, transportation type, receiving destination, equipment, supplies, and staffing.

## Records, Identification, and Care Information
Each resident should have identification and enough medical information to support continuity of care. When possible, send or make available face sheet or identifying information, medication information, current orders, allergies, code status, advance directives, provider contacts, responsible-party contacts, clinical precautions, equipment needs, and recent care information.

## Movement and Transportation
Skilled Nursing residents should move using the safest approved route and equipment. If elevators are cleared, staff should follow the plan for {{elevator_locations}}. If elevators are unavailable, staff should use approved stairwell evacuation equipment, transfer aids, or alternate movement procedures.

Residents may be staged in holding areas or at {{temporary_stopover_location}} while transportation and receiving placement are finalized. High-acuity residents should not be left without appropriate clinical supervision.

## Medications, Supplies, and Belongings
Staff should prepare medications, medication information, specialized supplies, personal equipment, assistive devices, and essential belongings as time and safety allow. Controlled substances should follow the separate medication procedure.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Skilled Nursing residents often need clinical assessment before movement.
- High-risk residents may need special transport, equipment, or staff accompaniment.
- Medical information and medication continuity are essential.
- Destination, handoff, and responsible-party updates must be tracked.
