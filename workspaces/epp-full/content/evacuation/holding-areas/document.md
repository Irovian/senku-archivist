# Holding Areas

## Purpose
This document describes how {{facility_name}} establishes and manages holding areas during evacuation.

## Holding Area Use
Holding areas are temporary locations where residents can be received, identified, monitored, triaged, prepared for transport, discharged to responsible parties, or moved to a stop-over point or receiving facility. The Incident Commander or Operations Section Chief should verify that each holding area is safe, accessible, staffed, and appropriate for the residents assigned to it.

When external staging is needed, {{temporary_stopover_location}} may be used if available and appropriate for the incident. Command may choose another location based on weather, hazard conditions, accessibility, space, toileting, security, resident needs, and emergency-services direction.

## Setup and Staffing
The Triage Unit Leader, Holding Area Unit Leader, or designee should establish each holding area, request staff, and confirm supplies. Holding areas may need basic first aid supplies, resident identification materials, tracking forms, linens, incontinence supplies, communication devices, portable oxygen from {{portable_oxygen_supply_location}}, and portable suction from {{portable_suction_location}} when needed.

Memory-care residents, residents at risk for elopement, residents needing isolation, and residents with high clinical needs may require separate space, additional staffing, or closer monitoring.

## Tracking and Communication
Holding area staff should track resident arrivals, departures, destinations, belongings, records, medications, equipment, accompanying staff, and family or physician notification status when applicable. The command center should be updated on capacity, staffing needs, supply needs, and barriers.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Holding areas are temporary staging and monitoring locations.
- A holding area should not receive residents until it is safe, staffed, and ready.
- Residents must be tracked when they arrive and when they depart.
- Memory-care, isolation, and high-acuity needs may require separate controls.
