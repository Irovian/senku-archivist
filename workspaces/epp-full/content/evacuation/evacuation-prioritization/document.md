# Evacuation Prioritization

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} prioritizes resident movement during evacuation.

## Priority Decision Factors
The Incident Commander should determine evacuation priority with input from Operations, Planning, Logistics, Safety/Security/Liaison, resident-care leaders, and emergency services when available. Prioritization should reflect immediate life-safety risk, resident acuity, mobility, cognitive status, treatment needs, available staff, route safety, elevator or stairwell access, holding-area capacity, transportation availability, and receiving-facility capability.

Evacuation priority may change as the incident changes. Severe weather, fire conditions, utility failure, structural concerns, hazardous materials, security threats, or lack of transportation may require a different order than the facility would use in a planned evacuation.

## General Approach
When conditions allow, leadership should use an orderly sequence that reduces risk to residents and staff. The sequence may consider ambulatory residents, non-ambulatory residents, higher acuity residents, residents who need specialized equipment, and residents at risk for elopement or distress.

If the evacuation is planned in advance of a predictable hazard, leadership may choose to move higher acuity residents or harder-to-place residents earlier so transportation, staff, records, medications, and receiving arrangements can be coordinated before conditions worsen.

## Holding Area and Receiving Capacity
Before directing another area to evacuate, command should verify that holding areas can receive additional residents and that movement will not create unsafe crowding. Receiving options should be checked through {{receiving_facility_partners}} or the related receiving-facility resource documents.

## Documentation
The command center should document the prioritization decision, the rationale, any changes made during the event, and resident groups or areas already evacuated. Detailed resident-by-resident tracking remains in the evacuation tracking tools.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Evacuation order is based on safety, resident needs, route conditions, and available support.
- Priority may change as the incident changes.
- Holding-area and receiving capacity must be checked before expanding movement.
- Staff should report resident needs that may affect safe evacuation.
