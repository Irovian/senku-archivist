# Medications and Federally Controlled Substances

## Purpose
This document describes how {{facility_name}} manages medications and federally controlled substances during evacuation.

## Medication Continuity
Residents should evacuate with the medications, medication information, and clinical instructions needed to maintain safe care when practical. Nursing leadership should identify medication needs for holding areas, transportation, stop-over use, receiving facilities, and residents who leave with responsible parties.

Medication preparation should consider resident identity, current orders, allergies, time-sensitive medications, refrigeration, medication administration records, controlled-substance requirements, and whether clinical staff will accompany the resident.

## Controlled Substances
Federally controlled substances should be managed by licensed or otherwise authorized staff according to law, pharmacy policy, and facility procedure. Controlled substances should not be sent casually with residents, families, or unassigned staff. If controlled substances must be moved, the facility should maintain chain-of-custody, count, documentation, secure transport, receiving handoff, and reconciliation.

## Packaging and Handoff
Medications sent with a resident should be labeled and connected to the resident's identifying information. Staff should communicate medication status to the receiving facility, responsible party, or transport staff as appropriate. If medications remain at the facility or cannot accompany the resident, staff should document the reason and notify the receiving care team.

## Documentation
Medication movement, controlled-substance counts, missing medication issues, pharmacy communications, and receiving-facility handoffs should be documented. Any discrepancy should be escalated to nursing leadership, pharmacy, and command.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Medication continuity is a resident-safety priority during evacuation.
- Controlled substances require secure handling, authorized staff, and documentation.
- Medication information should accompany or be available for each resident.
- Discrepancies must be reported immediately.
