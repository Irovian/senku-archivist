# Communications

## Purpose
This document describes how {{facility_name}} maintains communication during a full building evacuation so command, departments, resident-care areas, holding areas, labor pool staff, evacuation teams, emergency responders, residents, and responsible parties receive timely and consistent information.

## Communication Priorities
Evacuation communication should support life safety, resident tracking, staff assignments, transportation coordination, receiving placement, responsible-party updates, and documentation of major decisions. The command center should decide which communication methods are active, who is authorized to send evacuation instructions, and how urgent messages will be escalated when normal systems are unavailable.

The primary facility emergency contact is {{primary_emergency_contact_name}}. State long-term care ombudsman contact information is maintained as {{state_ltc_ombudsman_contact}} when that contact is needed for emergency communication or follow-up.

## Command Center Communication Network
The command center should establish and maintain communication with:

- Section Chiefs and command staff.
- Resident-care units or departments.
- Holding areas.
- The labor pool.
- Evacuation group leaders.
- Transportation staging and pick-up points.
- Emergency services and other outside response partners, when involved.

Messages should identify the affected area, action requested, reporting deadline, and callback or reply method. When communication volume is high, the command center should prioritize messages involving immediate resident safety, evacuation route changes, holding-area capacity, transportation arrival, receiving-facility acceptance, staff accountability, and unresolved hazards.

## Communication Methods
The facility should use the communication methods that remain functional and appropriate for the incident. Methods may include standard telephones, runners, portable radios, cell phones, text messaging, email, overhead or paging systems, written message forms, or other approved communication channels.

Staff should use assigned channels for evacuation instructions and avoid sending unapproved or conflicting messages. If a communication method fails, staff should report the failure through the next available method and continue using the command-approved backup process.

During a bomb threat or similar security event, staff should not use radios, cell phones, or other wireless devices in the affected area unless law enforcement or the Incident Commander confirms they may be used safely.

## Resident and Responsible-Party Updates
Resident and responsible-party information should be coordinated through the command center, Public Information Officer, resident-care leadership, or another assigned leader. Updates should be accurate, privacy-aware, and consistent with resident tracking information.

When a resident is moved to a holding area, stop-over point, family discharge location, or receiving facility, the responsible-party update should identify the resident's known or expected location when that information is available and appropriate to release. Staff should avoid making promises about timing, destination, or return to the facility until command has confirmed those details.

## Documentation
Important evacuation communications should be documented when they affect resident movement, destination, continuity of care, staffing, transportation, outside-agency coordination, family updates, or operational decisions. Documentation may occur through command logs, message forms, resident tracking tools, call logs, department status updates, or other approved incident records.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This document owns communication expectations specific to full building evacuation. The all-hazards communication documents describe broader internal, external, resident, family, and agency communication procedures. Resident tracking forms, receiving-facility lists, and detailed message templates remain separate tools for the later artifact pass.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Evacuation messages should come through the command-approved communication process.
- Command must be able to reach resident-care areas, holding areas, the labor pool, evacuation teams, transportation points, and outside responders.
- Resident and responsible-party updates must protect privacy and match verified tracking information.
- Communication failures should be escalated through backup methods.
- Wireless devices may be restricted during a bomb threat or security event.
