# Staff and Emergency Services

## Purpose
This document describes how {{facility_name}} coordinates staff and emergency services access during evacuation.

## Access and Movement Routes
Incident command should identify which routes are reserved for resident evacuation and which routes are available for staff, emergency responders, equipment movement, and supply movement. Staff and responders should avoid blocking resident movement routes, holding area access, transportation pickup points, and command center operations.

When elevators are available and cleared for use, route decisions should account for {{elevator_locations}} and the need to separate resident movement from responder or equipment movement when practical. When elevators are not available, command should identify safe stairs, ramps, exits, and {{alternate_vertical_transport_methods}}.

## Emergency Services Coordination
The Safety/Security/Liaison Officer or designee should coordinate with fire, EMS, law enforcement, emergency management, or other responders. Coordination should include hazard conditions, building access, staging areas, resident acuity, transportation needs, utility concerns, traffic flow, and whether responders are directing any part of the evacuation.

If emergency services establish an external command post, facility command should maintain communication with that post while continuing internal resident-care coordination.

## Staff Direction
Staff should follow assigned routes and avoid self-directed movement that conflicts with command decisions. Department leaders should account for staff in their areas, report missing or delayed staff, and request assistance through the command center or labor pool.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Resident movement routes, responder routes, and equipment routes may be different.
- Emergency responders may affect staging, traffic flow, route use, and evacuation timing.
- Staff should report route hazards or congestion immediately.
- Staff accountability remains a department and command responsibility.
