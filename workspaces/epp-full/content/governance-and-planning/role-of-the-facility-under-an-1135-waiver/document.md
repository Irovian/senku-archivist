# Role of the Facility under an 1135 Waiver

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} understands and organizes its role when an 1135 waiver is declared and emergency management officials direct resident care to another approved emergency location.

## Waiver Role
An 1135 waiver may be issued during a declared emergency when federal requirements are temporarily modified to support continued care. Under the Emergency Preparedness Program, {{facility_name}} plans for the possibility that its role under such a waiver may include delivering or supporting resident care in an emergency-designated alternate location.

This document does not grant authority to waive requirements or independently create an alternate care site. It explains how the facility prepares to coordinate if a waiver is issued, if emergency management officials identify an alternate care site, and if residents cannot be safely served through normal facility operations or existing receiving-facility arrangements.

When an 1135 waiver is not applicable, or when residents can be relocated to facilities with existing agreements, {{facility_name}} follows the Full Building Evacuation Plan and related transfer arrangements.

## Activation and Coordination
If emergency management officials direct or support the use of an alternate care site under waiver conditions, the facility coordinates the response through its Incident Command System. Facility leadership should confirm the scope of the waiver, the direction received from emergency management or health authorities, and the specific role the facility is expected to perform.

At a minimum, coordination should address:

- activation of the Incident Command System to manage the facility response;
- use of the Full Building Evacuation Plan to package, prepare, move, and track residents when relocation is required;
- activation of mutual aid plans, written agreements, memoranda of understanding, vendor arrangements, or supplier support when applicable;
- notification and coordination with local emergency responders, emergency management officials, local and state health departments, residents and responsible parties, staff, receiving partners, and public information channels as assigned;
- communication with the state health department or other designated authority during a declared emergency involving an 1135 waiver.

## Alternate Care Site Support
When residents are directed to an alternate care site, {{facility_name}} should assess what resources, staff, records, equipment, supplies, and communication support are needed to preserve resident safety and continuity of care. The facility may move needed resources with residents, request partner or vendor support, or rely on resources coordinated by emergency management, depending on the event and the direction received.

The facility's resource assessment should consider resident acuity, medication needs, medical equipment, mobility support, personal care needs, infection prevention, food and water, documentation supplies, privacy, communication methods, and any other items necessary for safe resident care. Detailed resource lists and movement procedures are maintained in the evacuation, resource, and incident command documents.

Staff should use appropriate personal protective equipment and follow infection prevention expectations when supporting care at an alternate care site.

## Staffing, Credentialing, and Transportation
Staffing decisions under waiver conditions should be coordinated through the Incident Command System and the facility's disaster staffing procedures. If a waiver affects licensure, credentialing, or the ability of individuals to assist outside their usual practice setting, the facility follows its emergency credentialing process and any direction issued by the applicable authority.

Transportation should be coordinated through the Full Building Evacuation Plan, emergency services, receiving partners, and available facility-owned or partner vehicles as appropriate. Transportation decisions should account for resident condition, needed equipment, medication and record transfer, staff accompaniment, and destination readiness.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic establishes the facility's policy-level role under an 1135 waiver. It should be read with the documents that contain the operational detail:

- Continuity of Operations describes how the facility evaluates service continuity, alternate placement, and receiving-site relationships.
- Full Building Evacuation Plan documents describe resident preparation, tracking, transportation, and transfer workflows.
- Communications describes internal and external notification, reporting, and public information boundaries.
- Resident Medical Information describes how medical records and protected resident information are preserved and transferred.
- Emergency Credentialing Program and disaster staffing documents describe staffing support when normal staffing models are disrupted.
- Resource and vendor documents identify supplies, equipment, transportation resources, and partner support.

## What Staff Should Understand
- An 1135 waiver is tied to declared emergency authority; the facility does not create the waiver on its own.
- The facility's role may include supporting resident care in an emergency-designated alternate location.
- Incident command coordinates waiver-related decisions, notifications, resource assessment, staffing, transportation, and documentation.
- Existing evacuation, communication, staffing, credentialing, records, and resource procedures still guide the operational work.
- If waiver conditions do not apply, the facility follows normal evacuation and transfer procedures.
