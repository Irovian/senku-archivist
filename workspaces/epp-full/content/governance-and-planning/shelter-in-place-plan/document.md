# Shelter in Place Plan

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} evaluates and manages shelter-in-place conditions during an emergency. It establishes the facility's policy-level expectations for keeping residents, staff, and essential operations protected when remaining in the building is safer than evacuation or relocation.

## Shelter-in-Place Role
Sheltering in place may be appropriate when an internal or external hazard makes movement unsafe, when transportation or receiving-site options are limited, or when residents can be protected more effectively by remaining inside the facility. The decision must consider the facility's hazard vulnerability analysis, resident population needs, available staff, building conditions, supplies, utilities, security, communications, and the expected duration of the event.

Shelter-in-place planning is part of the facility's all-hazards emergency preparedness program, but this document does not replace detailed response procedures. It provides the governance-level expectations that connect risk assessment, resident-service continuity, incident command, and related emergency procedures.

## Activation and Decision Criteria
Shelter-in-place actions are coordinated through the facility's emergency leadership structure. The Incident Commander or designee should determine whether sheltering in place is safer than evacuation based on the hazard, current resident needs, available resources, direction from emergency officials, and the facility's ability to maintain essential care.

When {{facility_name}} uses a code word, alert phrase, or other internal signal to activate emergency operations, staff should follow the facility's approved activation term: {{eop_activation_code_word}}.

The decision should consider:

- whether the hazard is inside the facility, outside the facility, or affecting the surrounding community;
- whether residents can remain safely in current rooms or need movement to safer interior areas;
- whether staffing, supplies, medications, food, water, oxygen, equipment, communications, and utilities can support continued care;
- whether doors, entrances, resident care areas, and critical operational areas can be secured and monitored;
- whether the expected event duration exceeds the facility's current resources or requires mutual aid, vendor support, transfer planning, or evacuation readiness.

If conditions change, leadership should reassess the shelter-in-place decision and prepare to adjust operations, reduce census, relocate residents within the building, or evacuate if remaining in place is no longer safe.

## Resident Safety and Essential Services
During shelter-in-place operations, resident safety and continuity of essential services remain the central priorities. Staff should continue resident care to the greatest extent possible while adapting routines to the emergency conditions.

The facility should account for residents with mobility limitations, cognitive impairment, medical equipment needs, oxygen dependence, infection prevention concerns, behavioral health needs, communication barriers, and other clinical or psychosocial risks. Residents should be monitored for changes in condition, distress, environmental exposure, hydration, nutrition, medication needs, and access to necessary support.

Leadership should identify which services must continue immediately, which services can be modified, and which services may need temporary alternative arrangements. Documentation should support continuity of care and should record major operational decisions, resident movement, service interruptions, and any protective actions taken.

## Staffing, Security, Utilities, and Resources
Shelter-in-place operations may require adjusted staffing assignments, extended shifts, staff support areas, restricted access, supply conservation, and close monitoring of building systems. Department leaders should report staffing, resource, and operational concerns through the emergency leadership structure so decisions can be coordinated across the facility.

Security and access control should be appropriate to the hazard. Staff may need to limit entry, monitor doors, direct visitors or responsible parties, protect resident care areas, and coordinate with emergency responders. If the event affects community access or transportation, leadership should plan for staff relief, food, rest areas, and continued communication with off-duty staff.

Utilities and resource readiness should be monitored throughout the event. Power, water, heating, cooling, oxygen systems, communications, sanitation, food service, laundry, medical supplies, fuel, and generator status should be evaluated against the expected duration of sheltering in place. Detailed utility failure procedures, generator information, emergency supply lists, and resource tracking tools are maintained in related operational and resource documents.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This document sets the policy-level framework for sheltering in place. It should be used with the documents that contain detailed operational steps:

- Continuity of Operations describes resident population needs, service continuity, and leadership continuity during disrupted operations.
- Risk Assessment Process describes how hazards are identified and used to shape emergency strategies.
- Communications describes internal alerts, external notifications, responsible-party information, and reporting expectations.
- Building lockdown, security, staff sheltering, staff family sheltering, staff pet sheltering, utility-loss, resource, generator, and emergency operations activation documents describe procedure-level actions.
- Evacuation documents describe the transition from sheltering in place to partial or full evacuation when the facility can no longer protect residents safely in place.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Shelter in place is a safety decision based on the hazard, resident needs, facility conditions, and available resources.
- The facility may begin sheltering in place and later change course if conditions become unsafe or essential services cannot be sustained.
- Resident safety, essential care, documentation, security, utilities, staffing, and communication must be managed together.
- Staff should follow the related operational procedures for lockdown, utilities, staffing, supplies, emergency activation, and evacuation readiness.
- Detailed forms, resource lists, and step-by-step response procedures live in the related operational documents, not in this governance topic.
