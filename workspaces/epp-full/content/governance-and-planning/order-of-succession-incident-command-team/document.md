# Order of Succession - Incident Command Team

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} maintains an order of succession for emergency leadership and incident command functions when usual leaders are unavailable.

## Succession Framework
The order of succession supports continuity of operations by identifying who may assume emergency leadership responsibilities during an activation. Succession planning helps the facility make timely decisions when an emergency occurs after hours, during weekends or holidays, during a staffing disruption, or when a designated leader cannot be reached.

The succession structure should identify the leadership roles that may assume responsibility for incident command and related command functions. It should be reviewed when leadership roles, staffing patterns, department structures, or incident command assignments change.

The facility-specific incident command succession titles are: {{incident_command_succession_titles}}.

## Incident Command Coverage
During emergency operations, succession expectations should support the Incident Commander role and other command functions needed for the event. Depending on the emergency, this may include public information, safety and security, operations, planning, logistics, finance, documentation, labor pool, triage, holding area, or other assigned roles.

The succession list does not replace the incident command job action sheets or role-specific procedures. It establishes who may assume leadership when needed; the detailed duties of each incident command role remain in the incident command documents.

## Maintaining the Succession List
{{facility_name}} maintains the order of succession through its organizational chart, staff roster, incident command roster, or other approved leadership resource. The list should be accessible to leaders who may need to activate the plan and should be protected from becoming outdated.

When an emergency is active, the person assuming a successor role should document the assumption of authority, communicate the role assignment to appropriate staff, and transition the role when a higher-authority or more appropriate leader becomes available.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic provides the governance-level succession expectation. It should be read with:

- Authority to Activate the Plan, which describes who may activate the emergency operations plan.
- Activation of the Incident Command System and incident command job action sheets, which describe procedure-level role activation.
- Continuity of Operations, which explains how succession supports continuity decisions.
- Command Center or incident command organizational chart resources, which maintain role assignments and contact details.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Succession planning ensures emergency leadership can continue when usual leaders are unavailable.
- The order of succession identifies who may assume authority; job action sheets describe what each role does.
- Successor role assignments should be communicated and documented during activation.
- Succession lists must be kept current as leadership roles and staffing structures change.
- Detailed incident command duties live in the incident command documents, not in this governance topic.
