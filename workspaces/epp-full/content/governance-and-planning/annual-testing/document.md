# Annual Testing

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} tests the emergency plan through drills, exercises, actual event review when applicable, documentation, and improvement tracking.

## Exercise Expectations
{{facility_name}} conducts emergency preparedness testing activities during the year to evaluate whether emergency procedures can be used in practice and whether staff understand their roles. Testing should be tied to the emergency plan, hazard vulnerability analysis, resident population needs, continuity priorities, communication plan, and related emergency procedures.

The annual testing cycle should include exercises that are appropriate to the facility's risk profile and regulatory expectations. Exercises may include community-based exercises, facility-based exercises, tabletop exercises, functional exercises, drills, or review of an actual emergency activation when the event meets the facility's criteria for exercise credit.

## Community-Based and Facility-Based Testing
When a community-based full-scale exercise is available and appropriate, {{facility_name}} should participate and document the facility's role, objectives, actions, partners, findings, and improvement needs.

When {{facility_name}} participates in a formal mutual aid plan, a mutual aid exercise involving healthcare facilities, local or regional partners, and emergency responders may support the community-based exercise expectation.

If no suitable community-wide full-scale opportunity is available, the facility should document its efforts to identify or join community exercise opportunities and conduct an appropriate facility-based exercise. The exercise should still evaluate the facility's emergency plan and should involve community partners or stakeholders when feasible.

The second testing activity may be an internal functional exercise, drill, tabletop exercise, or similar testing activity that challenges the emergency plan. Tabletop exercises should use a realistic emergency scenario and prompts that require participants to discuss decisions, communication, resident safety, staffing, resources, continuity, and recovery.

## Documentation and Improvement Tracking
The facility documents drills, tabletop exercises, functional exercises, actual emergency activations used for testing credit, and related improvement work using {{after_action_report_format}} or another approved documentation format.

Documentation should capture the exercise type, date, scenario or event, participants, objectives, decisions made, strengths, gaps, corrective actions, and plan revisions needed. Improvement tasks should be tracked until resolved or transferred to the appropriate program owner.

The role or committee responsible for reviewing, tracking, and assigning improvement tasks is {{after_action_improvement_owner}}.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic describes annual testing expectations. It should be read with:

- Training and Testing Program, which owns the overall program framework and review cycle.
- Training, which describes initial and annual training expectations.
- Risk Assessment Process, which informs exercise priorities and scenarios.
- Communications, incident command, all-hazards response, evacuation, recovery, and resource documents, which may be tested during drills and exercises.
- Appendix and evidence documents, which maintain exercise records, after-action reports, improvement plans, sign-in sheets, and related documentation.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Emergency preparedness testing is used to evaluate the plan, not only to satisfy a paperwork requirement.
- Exercises should reflect realistic hazards, resident needs, staffing conditions, communication needs, and continuity priorities.
- Actual emergencies may inform testing evidence and improvement work when documented appropriately.
- After-action review should identify strengths, gaps, corrective actions, and plan updates.
- Improvement tasks should be assigned, tracked, and revisited until resolved.
