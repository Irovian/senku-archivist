# Training

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} provides emergency preparedness training to employees, on-site contracted personnel, and volunteers in a way that matches their emergency responsibilities.

## Training Audience and Timing
Emergency preparedness training is provided initially and at least annually. Training should include new and existing staff, contracted personnel who work on site, and volunteers whose roles may affect resident safety or emergency operations.

Training should be appropriate to the person's expected role. Leadership, clinical staff, support departments, contracted service providers, volunteers, and incident command roles may need different levels of detail, but each should understand the emergency procedures and communication expectations that apply to their duties.

## Training Content
Training should address the facility's emergency preparedness program, emergency operations plan, relevant policies and procedures, communication plan, role expectations, and the response actions staff may need to take during an emergency. The training should connect to the facility's hazards, resident population, continuity priorities, and all-hazards response procedures.

Training may include review of emergency codes, incident command activation, internal and external communication expectations, resident safety priorities, shelter-in-place and evacuation concepts, utility disruption response, staffing expectations, documentation requirements, and the location of related emergency resources.

## Competency and Documentation
{{facility_name}} maintains documentation of emergency preparedness training. Documentation should show who received training, when training occurred, what content was covered, and whether the training was initial, annual, role-specific, corrective, or related to a significant update.

Staff knowledge should be demonstrated through methods appropriate to the training content. These methods may include quizzes, return demonstration, scenario discussion, drill participation, exercise evaluation, department review, or other facility-approved methods. Training results should inform improvement work when knowledge gaps or procedural issues are identified.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic describes training expectations. It should be read with:

- Training and Testing Program, which owns the overall training and testing framework.
- Annual Testing, which describes drills, exercises, after-action review, and improvement tracking.
- Communications, incident command, all-hazards response, evacuation, and resource documents, which provide content that staff may need to know by role.
- Appendix and evidence documents, which maintain sign-in sheets, competency checks, training materials, rosters, and other records.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Emergency preparedness training applies to staff, on-site contracted personnel, and volunteers based on their assigned responsibilities.
- Training occurs initially and at least annually.
- Training should be role-appropriate and tied to the facility's actual emergency procedures.
- The facility must maintain documentation of training and staff knowledge.
- Drill, exercise, and actual event findings should be used to improve future training.
