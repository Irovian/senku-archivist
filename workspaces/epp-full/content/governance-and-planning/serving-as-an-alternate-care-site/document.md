# Serving as an Alternate Care Site

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} prepares to serve as a receiving facility or alternate care site when another healthcare facility evacuates or when emergency conditions require the facility to support additional residents.

## Role as a Receiving or Alternate Care Site
{{facility_name}} may be asked to receive residents from another healthcare facility or to support care for additional residents during an emergency. This role is separate from the facility's role under an 1135 waiver. The waiver topic addresses emergency authority and waiver-specific direction; this document addresses the facility's readiness to evaluate whether it can safely receive, triage, track, and support incoming residents.

The facility's ability to serve in this role depends on current operating conditions, resident census, staffing, available beds or surge locations, equipment, supplies, utilities, infection prevention needs, and the care requirements of incoming residents. The facility should not accept a role that exceeds its ability to protect current residents, incoming residents, staff, and essential operations.

## Activation and Capacity Assessment
When {{facility_name}} is asked to receive residents or function as an alternate care location, the facility coordinates the decision through its emergency leadership structure. The Incident Commander or designee should assess the request, confirm the source and urgency of the request, and determine whether the facility can safely support the proposed role.

The capacity assessment should consider:

- current census, resident acuity, and care needs already present in the facility;
- available surge beds, rooms, holding areas, or other approved care locations;
- staffing levels, staff competencies, and supervision needed for additional residents;
- medical equipment, medications, supplies, food, water, linen, personal care items, and infection prevention resources;
- utility status, environmental safety, security, communications, transportation access, and documentation capacity;
- whether accepting incoming residents would compromise the facility's ability to maintain its own emergency operations.

If the facility cannot safely accept incoming residents, leadership should communicate that limitation through the assigned emergency communication process and, when appropriate, identify any support the facility can still provide.

## Incoming Resident Triage, Tracking, and Support
If {{facility_name}} accepts incoming residents, the facility should establish a controlled intake process. Incoming residents should be identified, triaged, assigned to an appropriate care area, and tracked according to the facility's emergency procedures. Staff should confirm essential resident information, medical documentation, medications, equipment needs, responsible-party information, and any immediate clinical or safety concerns.

Care for incoming residents should be integrated into facility operations without losing accountability for the facility's existing residents. The facility should maintain clear documentation of arrival, placement, condition, transfer source, responsible contacts, services provided, and any later movement or discharge.

Detailed intake forms, resident tracking workflows, medication reconciliation steps, medical record handling, and evacuation or transfer forms are maintained in the related operational documents rather than repeated here.

## Staffing and Credentialing Coordination
Serving as a receiving facility or alternate care site may require additional staffing, adjusted assignments, or support from staff associated with the evacuating facility. Staffing decisions should be coordinated through incident command, disaster staffing procedures, and applicable credentialing processes.

If outside staff, volunteers, or staff from an evacuating facility assist with care, {{facility_name}} should confirm their role, supervision, credentialing status, identification, and permitted activities before they provide services. The facility should follow its emergency credentialing process and any applicable direction from regulatory or emergency management authorities.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic establishes the governance-level expectations for serving as a receiving facility or alternate care site. It should be read with the documents that contain the operational detail:

- Continuity of Operations describes service continuity, receiving-site relationships, and alternate placement boundaries.
- Role of the Facility under an 1135 Waiver describes waiver-specific authority and emergency-management-directed alternate care.
- Communications describes internal and external notifications, public information boundaries, and emergency reporting.
- Resident Medical Information describes medical record protection, transfer, and confidentiality expectations.
- Disaster staffing and emergency credentialing documents describe staffing support, supervision, and role validation.
- Section B surge, all-hazards response, resource, and resident tracking documents describe operational procedures, forms, supplies, and detailed workflows.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Serving as a receiving facility or alternate care site is a capacity and safety decision, not an automatic obligation.
- Current resident safety and essential facility operations must be protected before additional residents are accepted.
- Incident command coordinates acceptance decisions, capacity assessment, intake, tracking, staffing, communication, and documentation.
- Incoming residents must be identified, triaged, placed, tracked, and supported through approved emergency procedures.
- Detailed surge procedures, intake forms, resource lists, and tracking tools live in related operational documents.
