# Disaster Staffing

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} evaluates staffing needs during emergency operations and maintains a policy-level framework for emergency staffing decisions.

## Staffing Readiness Role
Disaster staffing planning supports continuity of operations by helping the facility understand whether available personnel can sustain resident care, essential services, safety, communication, and recovery activities during disrupted conditions.

Emergency staffing needs may change quickly when an event affects staff availability, transportation, weather access, resident acuity, building conditions, utilities, supplies, evacuation readiness, infection prevention needs, or requests for assistance from other facilities. Staffing decisions should be coordinated through the facility's emergency leadership structure so departments are not making competing assumptions about available personnel.

## Workforce Assessment
When the emergency operations plan is activated or when staffing disruption is likely, leadership should assess the current workforce and anticipated staffing needs. This assessment should consider staff currently on site, staff expected for upcoming shifts, staff who may be delayed or unable to report, contracted or agency support, volunteers when applicable, department coverage, resident care needs, and staff fatigue.

The workforce assessment should help determine whether normal assignments can continue, whether staffing must be reassigned, whether off-duty staff should be contacted, whether outside staffing support is needed, whether staff support areas are needed, and whether resident services must be modified, deferred, transferred, or supported through alternate arrangements.

## Emergency Staffing Strategies
Emergency staffing strategies may include reassignment of staff, adjusted shift patterns, activation of off-duty staff notification processes, use of approved contracted or agency personnel, integration of authorized volunteers or designated healthcare professionals, department-level staffing prioritization, staff support planning, and coordination with incident command or emergency management when outside assistance is needed.

Staffing strategies should protect resident safety while also recognizing staff welfare. Staff may need rest, food, communication with family, mental health support, sleeping areas, transportation support, or other practical support during extended operations. Detailed staff sheltering, staff family sheltering, staff pet sheltering, labor pool, notification, and critical incident stress procedures are maintained in the related operational documents.

- Management of Staff During a Disaster, Notification of Off-Duty Staff, Staff Sheltering, Staff Family Sheltering, Staff Pet Sheltering, Labor Pool, and incident command documents, which contain procedure-level staffing actions.
- Training and Annual Testing, which help evaluate staff knowledge and emergency staffing readiness.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Emergency staffing decisions are coordinated through the facility's emergency leadership structure.
- Staffing needs should be assessed against resident needs, essential services, shift coverage, staff fatigue, and expected event duration.
- Staffing strategies may include reassignment, off-duty notification, contracted support, volunteers, or other approved support.
- Staff welfare affects continuity of operations during extended events.
- Detailed labor pool, notification, sheltering, and staff support procedures live in related operational documents.
