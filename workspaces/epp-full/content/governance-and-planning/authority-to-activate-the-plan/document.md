# Authority to Activate the Plan

## Purpose
This document explains who has authority to activate the emergency operations plan for {{facility_name}} and how activation authority is maintained when usual leaders are unavailable.

## Activation Authority
The emergency operations plan may be activated when an emergency, disaster, serious disruption, or credible threat requires coordinated response beyond normal department operations. Activation may occur for facility-based events, community-based events, weather events, utility failures, staffing disruptions, evacuation threats, shelter-in-place conditions, or other situations that require organized emergency leadership.

The Administrator, Chief Executive Officer, or other designated executive leader normally has authority to activate the plan. If those leaders are not available, activation authority passes to the highest-ranking designated leader on site or reachable through the facility's succession process.

## Incident Commander Role
When the plan is activated, the person assuming activation authority may also assume the Incident Commander role until relieved by a higher-authority or more appropriate designated leader. The Incident Commander has overall authority for coordinating the facility response, setting response priorities, assigning emergency roles, and ensuring that resident safety and essential operations remain the primary focus.

Activation authority includes the ability to initiate emergency notifications, direct department leaders to assess conditions, open or identify a command location, assign incident command functions, request internal or external support, and coordinate with emergency management or response partners when needed.

## Activation Assessment
After activation, leadership should assess current conditions and anticipated needs. This assessment should consider resident safety, staffing, building systems, utilities, supplies, communications, security, weather or hazard conditions, transportation access, partner support, and whether the facility can continue to shelter in place or must prepare for transfer, relocation, or evacuation.

The assessment should be documented through the facility's approved emergency assessment tools, incident command notes, department status reports, action plans, or other response documentation. Detailed forms and procedure-level actions belong in the related incident command, all-hazards response, recovery, and appendix documents.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic identifies the governance expectation for activating the emergency operations plan. It should be read with:

- Order of Succession - Incident Command Team, which identifies the succession structure when usual leaders are unavailable.
- Activation of the Emergency Operations Plan and Activation of the Incident Command System, which contain procedure-level activation steps.
- Continuity of Operations, which describes how delegated authority and succession support continuity decisions.
- Communications, which describes internal and external communication expectations during activation.
- Recovery and assessment documents, which contain the forms and documentation used to evaluate conditions after activation.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Activation authority must be clear before an emergency occurs.
- If usual leaders are unavailable, designated successor roles may activate the plan and assume incident command responsibilities.
- Activation begins a coordinated response structure; it is not limited to calling a meeting or sending a notification.
- Initial assessment should focus on resident safety, staffing, resources, building conditions, communications, and the ability to sustain essential operations.
- Detailed activation steps and forms live in related operational and incident command documents.
