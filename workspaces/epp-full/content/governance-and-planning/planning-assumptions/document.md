# Planning Assumptions

## Purpose
This document identifies the planning assumptions that guide the emergency preparedness program for {{facility_name}}. These assumptions help the facility plan for conditions that may affect residents, staff, services, resources, and continuity of operations during an emergency.

## Role of Planning Assumptions
Planning assumptions are not predictions. They are practical conditions the facility expects could occur and should be ready to manage. They help connect the facility's hazard vulnerability analysis, emergency strategies, continuity planning, shelter-in-place planning, evacuation planning, staffing plans, resource planning, and communication procedures.

The assumptions in this document should be reviewed when the emergency preparedness program is reviewed, when the facility's risk assessment changes, after exercises or actual events, and when changes in services, resident population, building systems, staffing, vendors, or community conditions could affect emergency response.

## Core Emergency Planning Assumptions
{{facility_name}} plans with the understanding that emergencies may occur without warning, at any time of day, on any shift, and during weekends or holidays. Emergency conditions may affect only the facility, the surrounding neighborhood, the broader community, the region, or the state.

The facility assumes that some emergencies may create conditions in which normal staffing, communications, transportation, utilities, supply delivery, vendor support, technology systems, resident movement, visitor access, or external assistance are limited or delayed. The facility also assumes that local or state officials may declare an emergency and may issue direction that affects facility operations.

During a community-wide or regional event, staff may have difficulty traveling to or from the facility. The facility may need to adjust staffing assignments, support staff who remain on site, communicate with off-duty staff, and coordinate staffing decisions through incident command.

## Resident and Service Continuity Assumptions
Emergency planning must account for the resident population served by {{facility_name}}, including residents with mobility limitations, cognitive impairment, complex medical needs, oxygen or equipment dependence, behavioral health needs, communication barriers, infection prevention concerns, or other support needs.

The facility assumes that some emergencies may interrupt normal services or require services to be modified, delayed, relocated, or supported through alternate arrangements. Leadership should evaluate which services must continue immediately, which services can be adjusted safely, and when conditions require transfer, relocation, receiving-facility support, or evacuation planning.

The facility may receive requests to support other healthcare facilities during an emergency, including requests for supplies, equipment, staffing, transportation assistance, or receiving-site support. Any such request must be evaluated against current resident safety, available resources, staffing, building conditions, and the facility's ability to maintain essential operations.

## Resource and Infrastructure Assumptions
The facility assumes that emergencies may affect utilities, backup systems, information systems, security, supply chains, transportation access, vendor availability, and other infrastructure needed to support resident care. Backup power systems and other critical systems may fail or may not support all normal operations.

The facility plans to maintain emergency supplies, resource awareness, and contingency strategies appropriate to its assessed risks and resident needs, including a planned shelter-in-place duration of {{planned_shelter_in_place_duration}} when conditions allow residents to be protected safely in place. The ability to remain self-sustaining depends on the type of event, duration, staffing, utility status, supply availability, building conditions, and whether residents can be protected safely in place.

If conditions exceed facility capability, leadership should reassess whether to continue sheltering in place, reduce census, request support, relocate residents within the facility, transfer residents, or evacuate.

## Coordination With Related Documents
Planning assumptions provide a shared foundation for related emergency preparedness documents. They should be read with:

- Risk Assessment Process, which identifies hazards and informs emergency strategies.
- Continuity of Operations, which describes how essential services and leadership continuity are maintained.
- Shelter in Place Plan, which describes the decision framework for remaining in the facility during an emergency.
- Serving as an Alternate Care Site, which describes readiness to evaluate requests to receive or support residents from another facility.
- Communications, Disaster Staffing, Loss of Utilities, resource, evacuation, and all-hazards response documents, which contain more specific procedures and tools.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Planning assumptions describe realistic conditions the facility may need to manage, not a fixed script for every emergency.
- Assumptions should be reviewed against the facility's hazards, resident population, services, staffing, building systems, vendors, and community conditions.
- Emergencies may occur without warning and may affect staffing, supplies, utilities, transportation, communications, technology, and external support.
- Resident safety and essential services remain the priority when assumptions become real event conditions.
- Detailed response actions live in related operational documents; this topic provides the shared planning foundation.
