# Loss of Utilities

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} plans at the governance level for utility disruptions that could affect resident safety, essential services, shelter-in-place capability, evacuation decisions, and emergency power readiness.

## Utility Readiness Role
Utility disruptions may affect power, water, heating, cooling, sewer or waste systems, communications, medical gases, fuel, information systems, elevators, kitchen operations, fire protection systems, or other building systems needed for resident care and safe operations.

{{facility_name}} addresses utility risks through its hazard vulnerability analysis, emergency preparedness policies and procedures, continuity planning, resource planning, maintenance activities, vendor coordination, and response procedures. Utility planning should account for both short interruptions and extended disruptions that may require conservation, alternate processes, outside support, resident relocation within the building, transfer, or evacuation.

## Subsistence and Essential Services
Utility planning should support the subsistence needs of residents and staff whether the facility shelters in place or evacuates. Planning should consider food, water, medical and pharmaceutical supplies, safe temperatures, emergency lighting, fire detection and suppression systems, sewage and waste disposal, communications, and any other systems needed to maintain resident health and safety.

When a utility disruption occurs, leadership should assess whether essential services can continue safely, which services must be modified, whether backup systems are operating as expected, whether vendors or emergency partners should be contacted, and whether residents can remain safely in place.

## Emergency and Standby Power
Emergency and standby power systems are maintained based on the emergency plan and applicable life safety and health care facility requirements. Generator location, inspection, testing, maintenance, and fuel planning should be managed through the facility's approved maintenance, life safety, and emergency preparedness processes.

If {{facility_name}} maintains an onsite fuel source for emergency generators, the facility should maintain a plan for keeping emergency power systems operational during an emergency unless conditions require evacuation. Fuel planning should account for expected duration, vendor availability, delivery access, generator load, conservation decisions, and the conditions under which continued sheltering in place is no longer safe.

## Decision-Making During Utility Disruption
Utility disruption decisions should be coordinated through the emergency leadership structure. Leadership should consider resident vulnerability, clinical equipment dependence, temperature exposure, sanitation, lighting, communication capability, food service, medication storage, staff capacity, vendor support, emergency responder guidance, and whether the facility can continue to meet resident needs.

The governance expectation is to maintain utility contingency planning and escalation criteria. Detailed step-by-step response actions for specific utility failures remain in the related all-hazards response, hazard-specific, maintenance, resource, generator, and recovery documents.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic should be read with:

- Risk Assessment Process and Planning Assumptions, which identify utility risks and planning expectations.
- Shelter in Place Plan and Continuity of Operations, which describe how utility status affects resident protection and service continuity.
- Managing Utilities During a Disaster and specific utility-loss procedures, which contain operational response steps.
- Generator Information, Vendor Sources of Fuel, emergency supply lists, and maintenance records, which contain resource and technical detail.
- Evacuation and recovery documents, which describe decisions and actions when utility disruption exceeds facility capability.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Utility disruption planning protects resident safety, essential services, and shelter-in-place capability.
- Loss of utilities may require conservation, alternate procedures, vendor support, relocation, transfer, or evacuation.
- Emergency power readiness includes generator maintenance, testing, and fuel planning.
- Leadership should reassess utility status throughout an event and escalate when residents cannot be protected safely in place.
- Detailed utility response procedures and technical records live in related operational, resource, maintenance, and recovery documents.
