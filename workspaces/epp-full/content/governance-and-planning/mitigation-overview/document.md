# Mitigation Overview

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} uses risk assessment findings to guide mitigation activities that reduce the impact of emergencies on residents, staff, services, and facility operations.

## Mitigation Role
Mitigation is the planning work that reduces the likelihood, severity, or operational impact of emergency events before they occur. It connects the hazard vulnerability analysis to practical improvement work, resource planning, partner arrangements, staff readiness, and continuity strategies.

{{facility_name}} uses the results of its risk-assessment process to identify hazards that require mitigation attention. Leadership should consider the highest-priority risks, the resident population served, services provided, building and utility vulnerabilities, staffing risks, vendor dependencies, communication needs, transportation needs, and lessons learned from drills, exercises, or actual events.

## Mitigation Priorities
Mitigation priorities should be reviewed as part of the emergency preparedness program and whenever new risk information becomes available. Priority setting should consider both facility-based hazards and community-based hazards, including events that could affect staffing, utilities, access to the building, resident movement, supply delivery, communications, security, or continuity of care.

Mitigation activities may include maintaining emergency supplies, strengthening communication and notification methods, improving staff readiness, updating procedures, validating vendor and partner arrangements, reviewing evacuation or receiving-facility options, improving building or utility resilience, and correcting gaps identified through exercises or actual emergencies.

The mitigation overview does not require every improvement item to be listed in this topic. Detailed mitigation evidence may be maintained in the hazard vulnerability analysis, annual review records, improvement plans, vendor files, resource lists, maintenance records, training records, exercise documentation, and related operational documents.

## Documentation and Follow-Through
Mitigation work should be documented well enough to show what risk was identified, what action was selected, who owns follow-up, and whether the action was completed, deferred, or transferred to another program owner. Documentation may be maintained through the facility's emergency preparedness committee records, annual analysis, after-action improvement plan, maintenance records, administrative tracking tools, or other approved evidence files.

When mitigation work cannot be completed immediately, leadership should determine whether interim controls, alternate procedures, partner support, resource staging, or additional monitoring are needed until the issue is resolved.

## Coordination With Related Documents
This topic provides the governance-level mitigation framework. It should be read with:

- Risk Assessment Process, which identifies and prioritizes hazards.
- Planning Assumptions, which describe the conditions the facility plans to manage.
- Continuity of Operations, Shelter in Place Plan, Disaster Staffing, Loss of Utilities, and evacuation documents, which translate mitigation priorities into specific planning areas.
- Training and Annual Testing, which help reveal gaps that may become mitigation actions.
- Resource, vendor, maintenance, appendix, and evidence documents, which contain the detailed records supporting mitigation work.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Mitigation turns risk-assessment findings into practical preparedness work.
- The highest-priority hazards should influence supplies, staffing, communication, training, vendor arrangements, utility readiness, and response procedures.
- Exercise and actual-event findings should feed mitigation and improvement planning.
- Detailed mitigation records may live in evidence files and related operational documents.
- Mitigation is reviewed over time; it is not a one-time checklist.
