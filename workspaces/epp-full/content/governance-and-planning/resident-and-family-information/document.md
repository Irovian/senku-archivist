# Resident and Family Information

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} shares appropriate emergency-planning information with residents and their families, representatives, or responsible parties.

## Information Sharing Approach
{{facility_name}} provides residents and responsible parties with emergency-planning information in a format the facility determines is appropriate for admission, orientation, ongoing reference, and emergency use. The shared information should help residents and families understand the facility's emergency planning approach, how the facility communicates during disasters, what alternate contact methods may be used when normal channels fail, and where additional instructions may be posted or provided.

The facility may use a resident and responsible-party emergency communications guide, admission materials, resident handbook language, family notification resources, website updates, posted instructions, or another appropriate method to share this information. The method should be practical for the resident population, understandable to responsible parties, and consistent with the facility's broader communications plan.

## Content of Resident and Family Guidance
Resident and family emergency-planning information should describe, at a high level, what residents and responsible parties can expect during an emergency. The guidance should address how the facility may communicate about a facility emergency or community disaster, how responsible parties can attempt to contact the facility if routine telephone service is disrupted, and how the facility may provide alternate updates when normal communication channels are not available.

The shared guidance should also set appropriate expectations. It should not promise immediate individualized updates when conditions make that impossible, and it should not replace the facility's incident command, evacuation, resident tracking, or medical information procedures.

## Boundaries With Incident Communications
This topic stays focused on emergency plan information shared before an event and on the standing communication expectations residents and families should understand. Incident-specific notifications, resident status updates, evacuation destination reporting, reunification instructions, and medical information release are handled through the related communications, evacuation, incident command, and resident medical information documents.

When an event occurs, staff should follow assigned roles and approved communication processes. Family-facing information should be clear, consistent, and coordinated so residents and responsible parties receive reliable updates without disrupting response operations.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Residents and responsible parties should receive emergency-planning information before a disaster occurs.
- Shared information should be practical, readable, and consistent with the facility's broader communications plan.
- Family-facing guidance should explain alternate contact expectations without overpromising real-time individualized updates.
- Family-facing guidance does not replace incident command, evacuation, or status-reporting procedures.
- Questions that require operational detail should be routed to the related communications, resident medical information, evacuation, or response documents.
