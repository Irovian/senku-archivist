
# Wildfire

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} prepares for and responds to wildfire threats, smoke, ash, evacuation advisories, road closures, and related air-quality or access disruptions.

## Preparedness and Monitoring
Leadership should monitor local fire conditions, air-quality information, emergency management guidance, road closures, and evacuation advisories when wildfire risk may affect the facility. Departments should review shelter-in-place and evacuation plans, supply levels, transportation resources, communication methods, and critical resident needs.

## Smoke or Precautionary Threat Phase
When smoke, ash, or nearby fire conditions may affect the facility, staff should bring residents indoors, close windows, limit outdoor activities, and report respiratory concerns. Leadership should evaluate whether to adjust ventilation, issue respiratory protection or other protective measures, and protect residents with respiratory vulnerability.

Maintenance or assigned staff should reduce smoke and ash entry where practical, evaluate HVAC operations, check combustible materials near the building, and support access control to limit unnecessary traffic.

## Evacuation Threat Phase
Because healthcare evacuation can take longer than community evacuation, leadership should coordinate early with emergency management, transportation partners, receiving facilities, and responder agencies. Incident command should determine whether to reduce census, relocate high-risk residents, prepare medical records and medications, or begin evacuation.

## Recovery
Recovery includes resident reassessment, air-quality monitoring, cleaning smoke or ash contamination, HVAC and utility review, supply replacement, communication with residents and representatives, cost tracking, and after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Bring residents indoors when smoke or ash threatens the facility.
- Report respiratory distress, access problems, or supply concerns quickly.
- Follow leadership direction on ventilation, masking, relocation, or evacuation.
- Keep resident records, medications, and equipment ready if evacuation becomes likely.
