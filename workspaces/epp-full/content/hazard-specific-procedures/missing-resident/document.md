
# Missing Resident

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when a resident cannot be located and elopement or unsafe absence is suspected.

## Immediate Actions
When a resident is believed to be missing, staff should alert the nurse in charge or supervisor, conduct a quick search of the immediate area, and obtain the resident's last known location, time last seen, clothing, mobility status, cognitive status, medical risks, and likely destinations.

If the resident is not located promptly, leadership should activate the missing resident response, assign search areas, and determine whether building lockdown, incident command, police notification, family or representative notification, and regulatory notification are needed.

## Search Process
Staff should search resident rooms, bathrooms, closets, empty beds, common areas, stairwells, exits, outdoor areas, parking areas, and other likely locations. Search teams should report completed areas and findings to the command center or assigned leader.

A current photograph and missing resident information sheet should be provided to search teams and law enforcement when available. Staff should consider weather, time of day, medical needs, cognition, prior elopement history, and possible transportation routes.

## Notifications and Resident Care
Leadership should notify law enforcement without delay when risk factors or search findings warrant outside assistance. Nursing should document resident status, actions, notifications, and medical record entries. The resident's representative should be notified through the assigned communication process.

## Return and Recovery
When the resident is found, nursing should assess the resident for injury, exposure, distress, and medical needs. Leadership should stop the search, notify involved parties, complete incident reporting, evaluate root causes, update care planning, and complete after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report a potentially missing resident immediately.
- Search assigned areas thoroughly and report results.
- Use the missing resident information sheet to share accurate details.
- Do not delay police notification when risk is present.
- Document actions and resident condition.
