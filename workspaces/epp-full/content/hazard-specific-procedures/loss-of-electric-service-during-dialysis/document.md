# Loss of Electric Service During Dialysis

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} protects residents receiving dialysis or dependent on dialysis-related services when electric service is interrupted, unstable, unavailable, or unable to support dialysis treatment safely.

## Recognition and Activation
Staff should report loss of power, partial power loss, generator transfer problems, dialysis equipment shutdown, water treatment equipment shutdown, alarm conditions, loss of emergency outlets, or any power condition affecting dialysis treatment immediately.

Dialysis staff, contracted dialysis providers, or dialysis clinical support should be contacted through {{dialysis_staff_contact_process}}. If residents are receiving dialysis at the time of power loss, clinical staff should follow dialysis provider direction for treatment interruption, rinse-back, disconnection, monitoring, transfer, or emergency medical services.

The Loss of Electric Service and Loss of Emergency Generator Power procedures should also be activated when the outage affects building power, emergency power, generator function, or other power-dependent systems.

## Dialysis Safety Priorities
The command center or assigned clinical leader should identify residents receiving dialysis, residents scheduled for dialysis, residents who may miss treatment, and residents whose condition may worsen if treatment is delayed.

Clinical staff should assess resident condition, treatment status, access safety, fluid status, medication timing, diet/fluid restrictions, transportation needs, and whether alternate dialysis arrangements are required.

Transfer, alternate treatment, or receiving-provider arrangements are described by {{dialysis_resident_transfer_arrangements}}.

## Emergency Power Assessment
Expected generator transfer or emergency power availability for dialysis-supporting systems is {{dialysis_generator_transfer_time}}. Areas and equipment supported by emergency power include {{emergency_power_supported_areas_equipment}}.

Critical power-dependent dialysis equipment, water treatment equipment, refrigeration, communication systems, or resident-care equipment requiring priority assessment include {{critical_power_dependent_equipment}}.

Maintenance, dialysis staff, and the command center or assigned leader should confirm whether dialysis equipment may be safely operated on available emergency power. Staff should not connect dialysis equipment to temporary power sources unless the equipment, power source, and connection method have been approved by qualified personnel.

## Operating During Power Loss
If dialysis cannot continue safely, clinical staff should stop or modify treatment according to dialysis provider direction, monitor residents, arrange transfer or alternate dialysis, and document missed or interrupted treatment.

The command center or assigned leader should coordinate transportation, receiving-provider communication, resident and representative updates, medication and diet coordination, and continuity of clinical documentation.

If power loss affects water supply, water quality, sewer function, oxygen/vacuum, communications, heating, cooling, or fire protection, the related procedure should also be considered.

## Recovery
Before dialysis services resume, dialysis staff or the contracted provider should confirm that power is stable, required equipment has been checked, water treatment equipment is operating safely, alarms are cleared, and residents can be treated safely.

Departments should document resident impacts, interrupted treatments, transfers, missed treatments, equipment issues, generator or power problems, vendor actions, and follow-up needed with dialysis providers.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Power loss during dialysis is a clinical escalation event.
- Dialysis staff or the contracted dialysis provider should direct treatment interruption and restart decisions.
- Emergency power availability does not automatically mean dialysis equipment may be used.
- Resident transfer or alternate dialysis arrangements may be needed quickly.
- Building electric-service and generator procedures remain active companions to this procedure.
