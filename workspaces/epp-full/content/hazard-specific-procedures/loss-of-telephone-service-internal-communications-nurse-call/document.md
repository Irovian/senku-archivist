# Loss of Telephone Service / Internal Communications / Nurse Call

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} maintains internal communication, staff notification, resident call support, and emergency escalation when telephone service, internal communication systems, or nurse call systems are unavailable, unreliable, or expected to fail.

## Recognition and Activation
Staff should report loss of telephone service, nurse call failure, pager or wireless communication failure, overhead paging failure, radio failure, network-supported phone disruption, or inability to reach essential departments immediately.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the disruption affects resident call response, staff assignments, emergency notifications, family communication, vendor contact, fire/life safety monitoring, elevator or utility response, or other procedures that rely on communications.

If resident call systems are unavailable, nursing leadership should initiate alternate call-light monitoring, increased rounding, unit-level communication checks, and resident notification as appropriate.

## Alternate Internal Communication Methods
Backup communication equipment is located or obtained through {{internal_communication_equipment_locations}}. Internal wireless, pager, phone, or similar systems include {{internal_wireless_communication_systems}}.

Power-fail or emergency phone locations and numbers include {{power_fail_phone_locations}}. Public, pay, or other outgoing-call phone locations include {{public_pay_phone_locations}}.

Portable radios may be obtained through {{portable_radio_source_location}}. Backup or emergency cell phones may be obtained through {{backup_cell_phone_source_location}}. Satellite phone equipment, when available for internal use, is located at {{satellite_phone_location}}.

Staff should use the communication method assigned by the command center or assigned leader and should avoid creating conflicting informal channels unless directed.

## Nurse Call Downtime Operations
Nursing should identify residents who rely heavily on nurse call systems, require frequent observation, have high fall risk, or may be unable to communicate needs without assistance.

When nurse call is down, units should use approved alternate methods such as increased rounding, in-room checks, room-to-room communication, portable bells or other facility-approved signaling methods, assigned hallway coverage, and direct staff notification.

Resident safety rounds, response delays, critical resident needs, equipment failures, and escalation actions should be documented according to facility downtime procedures.

## External Escalation and Recovery
If the communication outage affects emergency response, resident safety, fire watch, medical gas, dialysis, utility restoration, vendor response, or required reporting, the command center or assigned leader should use available backup communication methods to contact the appropriate parties.

Primary emergency contact escalation should follow {{primary_emergency_contact_name}} or the current emergency contact process.

After service is restored, departments should verify that phones, nurse call, radios, wireless devices, pagers, overhead paging, and backup communication devices are working. Returned backup devices should be inventoried, recharged, repaired, and stored using {{backup_communication_device_return_process}}.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report communication or nurse call failures immediately.
- Nurse call downtime requires active monitoring and alternate resident communication methods.
- Use assigned backup communication methods so the response remains coordinated.
- Fire watch, utilities, dialysis, oxygen/vacuum, and evacuation actions may depend on backup communications.
- Document resident impacts, communication failures, device use, and restoration problems.
