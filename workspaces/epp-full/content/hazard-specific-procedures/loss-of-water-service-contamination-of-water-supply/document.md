# Loss of Water Service / Contamination of Water Supply

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds to loss of water service, suspected water contamination, confirmed water contamination, or conditions that make normal potable or non-potable water use unsafe or unavailable.

## Recognition and Activation
Water service loss or contamination may affect drinking water, hand hygiene, food preparation, sanitation, laundry, clinical services, heating and cooling systems, boilers, fire protection, and resident care. Staff should report water pressure loss, discoloration, odor, taste changes, public water advisories, suspected contamination, broken water lines, or sewer cross-contamination concerns immediately.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the situation requires water conservation, bottled or bulk water, alternate sanitation methods, resident relocation, regulatory notification, fire watch, service modification, or evacuation. The facility should notify {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority when required by the event, duration, resident impact, fire protection impact, or facility policy.

## Water Supply Assessment
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess the cause, expected duration, affected building areas, domestic water valves, water heaters, boilers, pumps, fire protection systems, and other equipment that may be damaged or unsafe without water. The number of domestic water supply lines serving the building is {{water_supply_line_count}}.

Expected potable water use under restricted conditions is {{potable_water_daily_gallons}} per day. Potable water sources include {{potable_water_sources}}. Potable water vendors or alternate sources include {{potable_water_vendors}}. Non-potable water sources for building operations, sanitation, or fire protection support include {{nonpotable_water_sources}}. Non-potable water vendors or alternate sources include {{nonpotable_water_vendors}}.

## Water Conservation and Service Modification
The command center or assigned leader should direct departments to conserve water and prioritize use for resident hydration, medication administration, food preparation, hand hygiene, infection prevention, sanitation, and essential clinical needs. Departments should stop or reduce nonessential water use when needed.

{{dining_resource_owner_title}} should coordinate drinking water, food preparation, dishwashing alternatives, disposable supplies, and safe food-service modifications. {{environmental_resource_owner_title}} should coordinate housekeeping, sanitation, laundry, linen substitution, waste handling, and alternate cleaning processes. Disposable linen sources, when needed, include {{disposable_linen_source}}.

## Contaminated Water Controls
When water may be contaminated, staff should prevent use of unsafe water until the source is cleared. Controls may include posting affected fixtures, shutting off drinking fountains or connected dispensers, restricting ice and beverage machines, using bottled or bulk potable water, changing filters, flushing lines, and following public health or utility guidance before returning systems to service.

If water loss affects the fire sprinkler system or other fire protection systems, the command center or assigned leader should determine whether fire watch, fire department notification, regulatory reporting, or related fire protection procedures are required.

## Resident Care and Department Coordination
Nursing and clinical leaders should identify residents whose care depends on water availability, including hydration support, wound care, infection prevention, dialysis-related needs, medication administration, and equipment cleaning. Residents who cannot be safely supported under water restrictions should be considered for relocation, transfer, or alternate treatment arrangements.

Departments should document water-use restrictions, water deliveries, alternate sources, resident-care impacts, service limitations, vendor contacts, regulatory notifications, equipment shutdowns, sanitation changes, and recovery actions.

## Recovery
Before normal water use resumes, responsible staff should confirm that the water source is safe, affected equipment has been checked, required flushing or filter changes are complete, advisories have been lifted or addressed, and departments understand when normal service may restart. Remaining damage, costs, supply use, resident-care impacts, and lessons learned should be included in recovery documentation.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Do not use water that may be contaminated until the facility clears it for use.
- Potable water must be prioritized for resident hydration, medications, food preparation, and infection prevention.
- Non-potable water may support some building operations but must not be treated as drinking water.
- Water loss can affect fire protection, sanitation, dining, laundry, clinical care, and equipment.
- Recovery requires confirming water safety before normal use resumes.
