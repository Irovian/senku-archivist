
# Aggressive or Violent Behavior Guide

## Purpose
This guide describes how {{facility_name}} staff should respond when a resident, visitor, staff member, contractor, or other person becomes aggressive, threatening, or violent.

## Immediate Staff Response
Staff should stay calm, create distance, keep an exit route available, and call for assistance. Staff should avoid arguing, crowding the person, touching the person without need, blocking the person's exit, or making statements that escalate the situation.

If there is an immediate threat to life or serious injury, staff should call 911 or activate the emergency notification process. Residents, visitors, and staff not involved should be moved away from the area when safe.

## De-escalation and Safety
When de-escalation is safe, staff should use a low voice, simple statements, respectful language, and clear limits. Staff should listen for immediate needs and offer choices when appropriate. If de-escalation is not working or weapons are suspected, staff should withdraw and wait for trained assistance or law enforcement.

## Leadership Response
Leadership should assess the risk, assign staff to protect nearby residents, determine whether incident command is needed, and coordinate with nursing, security, maintenance, police, behavioral health resources, or other support. Nursing should assess involved residents for injury, distress, triggers, and follow-up care needs.

## Documentation and Recovery
The facility should document the event, injuries, notifications, response actions, resident assessments, and follow-up steps. Recovery may include staff debriefing, care-plan review, visitor restrictions, repair of damage, and after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Keep yourself and residents safe first.
- Call for help early.
- Do not argue, crowd, or challenge the person.
- Leave the area if weapons or serious violence are possible.
- Document facts, not assumptions.
