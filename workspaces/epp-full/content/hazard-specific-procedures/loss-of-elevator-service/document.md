# Loss of Elevator Service

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when one or more elevators are unavailable, unsafe, occupied by trapped passengers, or unable to support resident movement, staff movement, supply delivery, or evacuation support.

## Recognition and Activation
Staff should report elevator outage, elevator entrapment, abnormal elevator operation, elevator alarms, doors that will not open or close properly, water or fire conditions affecting elevator use, or any elevator-related injury immediately.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the event requires elevator rescue support, emergency services, elevator vendor response, alternate vertical transport, resident relocation, modified meal or supply delivery, visitor direction, admission/service modification, or activation of related electric, generator, fire, flood, or evacuation procedures.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by the event, duration, resident impact, injury, entrapment, evacuation impact, or facility policy.

## Immediate Entrapment Response
If anyone is inside an affected elevator, staff should reassure occupants, keep communication open when possible, determine whether anyone is injured or medically distressed, and notify the command center or assigned leader immediately.

Staff should not attempt elevator rescue unless trained and authorized. If occupants are injured, medically unstable, or rescue cannot be managed safely through the elevator vendor or authorized personnel, emergency services should be contacted.

## Elevator Service Assessment
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess affected elevators, power status, emergency power interface, fire alarm or sprinkler impacts, water intrusion, vendor status, expected downtime, and safe use of remaining elevators.

Elevator locations or service areas include {{elevator_locations}}. The elevator service or emergency vendor contact is {{elevator_service_contact}}. Alternate vertical transport methods include {{alternate_vertical_transport_methods}}.

## Operational Adjustments
The command center or assigned leader should determine whether resident movement, meal delivery, medication delivery, supply movement, laundry movement, visitor access, staff movement, or evacuation routes are affected.

Departments should plan to bring essential care, supplies, meals, and services to residents when vertical movement is restricted. Residents who cannot be safely supported on affected floors may need relocation, transfer, or additional monitoring.

Public-facing areas should be posted or staffed when elevators are unavailable so residents, visitors, vendors, responders, and staff know which routes or elevators may be used.

## Recovery
Elevators should return to service only after maintenance, the elevator vendor, emergency responders, or other authorized personnel confirm they are safe. Maintenance should verify related power, fire alarm, water, and control-system issues before normal use resumes.

Departments should document entrapments, resident-care impacts, delayed appointments, supply-delivery changes, vendor actions, repairs, and lessons learned.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report elevator problems immediately.
- Keep trapped occupants calm and connected with help; do not attempt unauthorized rescue.
- Elevator loss can affect resident movement, supply delivery, evacuation, meals, laundry, and appointments.
- Alternate vertical transport should be directed through the command center or assigned leader.
- Elevator keys and evacuation team role details belong in resource/list and evacuation documents.
