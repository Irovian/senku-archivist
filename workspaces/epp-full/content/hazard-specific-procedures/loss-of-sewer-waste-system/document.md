# Loss of Sewer / Waste System

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when sewer service, wastewater removal, waste disposal, or sanitation systems are disrupted or unsafe.

## Recognition and Activation
Sewer or waste system disruption may involve toilet backups, loss of flushing capability, sewer line damage, pump failure, waste-hauling interruption, contaminated areas, sewage odor, standing wastewater, or inability to safely remove waste from the facility.

Staff should report sewer backups, waste overflow, sanitation hazards, damaged fixtures, unusual odors, or loss of waste disposal service immediately. The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the event requires restroom restrictions, alternate toileting, waste storage, vendor support, infection prevention controls, regulatory notification, resident relocation, evacuation, or related water-service procedures.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by the event, duration, resident impact, sanitation risk, environmental impact, or facility policy.

## System Assessment
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess the cause, expected duration, affected fixtures, affected building areas, water supply implications, pumps, sewer lines, waste disposal routes, and vendor or municipal support needs.

Sewer, plumbing, waste-hauling, or sanitation vendor contacts include {{sewer_waste_vendor_contacts}}. If flushing toilets by non-potable water is approved and safe, non-potable water sources include {{nonpotable_water_sources}}.

{{environmental_resource_owner_title}} should coordinate sanitation controls, waste handling, contaminated area cleanup, housekeeping modifications, and waste storage actions.

## Sanitation and Alternate Toileting
The command center or assigned leader should identify which restrooms, fixtures, or areas may be used and which must be restricted. Unsafe fixtures should be posted or taken out of service until cleared.

Alternate toileting supplies, portable toilets, commodes, sanitation bags, or related sources include {{portable_toilet_sources}}. Temporary waste storage should occur at {{waste_storage_location}} or another location approved by the command center or assigned leader.

Waste should be contained, labeled when needed, secured from resident and visitor access, and handled using infection prevention and environmental services precautions. Staff should use waterless hand hygiene or other approved hand hygiene methods when normal sinks are unavailable.

## Resident Care and Department Coordination
Nursing and department leaders should identify residents who need toileting assistance, additional hygiene support, relocation, transfer, or modified care because of sewer or waste disruption. Dining, nursing, environmental services, and maintenance should coordinate sanitation, hand hygiene, linen use, disposable supplies, and waste movement.

If the sewer or waste problem is related to water loss, water contamination, flooding, power failure, or building damage, the related procedure should also be activated.

## Recovery
Before normal sewer or waste operations resume, responsible staff should confirm that affected systems are safe, repairs or vendor actions are complete, contaminated areas are cleaned, fixtures are cleared for use, and departments understand which restrooms or disposal routes may reopen.

Remaining sanitation impacts, resident-care impacts, vendor actions, repair needs, supplies used, and lessons learned should be documented for recovery.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report sewer, wastewater, or waste disposal problems immediately.
- Unsafe fixtures or contaminated areas should not be used until cleared.
- Alternate toileting and waste storage must be coordinated and secured.
- Hand hygiene and infection prevention remain priorities during sewer or waste disruption.
- Water contamination and routine vendor-list details remain in their dedicated documents.
