
# Volcanic Eruption

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} prepares for and responds to volcanic activity, ashfall, air-quality threats, evacuation advisories, and related utility or access disruption when this hazard is applicable to the facility's risk profile.

## Monitoring and Activation
Leadership should monitor emergency management and official hazard guidance when volcanic activity may affect the region. Incident command should be activated when advisory, watch, warning, ashfall, access, utility, respiratory, structural, or evacuation concerns may affect residents or operations.

## Protective Actions
Staff should bring residents indoors, close windows and doors, keep residents away from ash or fumes, and report respiratory symptoms, eye irritation, access problems, or building concerns. Maintenance or assigned staff should evaluate HVAC operation, air intake, doors, windows, generators, utilities, roof loading, and ash-related equipment risks.

Residents with respiratory vulnerability should be monitored closely. Outdoor travel and vehicle use should be limited when ashfall or visibility creates safety risk.

## Evacuation and Coordination
Leadership should coordinate with emergency management, responders, transportation partners, and receiving facilities if evacuation is advised or continued occupancy becomes unsafe. Resident records, medications, equipment, and tracking should be prepared according to the evacuation plan.

## Recovery
Recovery includes resident reassessment, ash and debris cleanup, structural and utility inspection, HVAC review, supply replacement, documentation, cost tracking, communication with residents and representatives, and after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Bring residents indoors and reduce ash or fume exposure.
- Report respiratory symptoms and building concerns quickly.
- Follow evacuation or sheltering direction from leadership and authorities.
- Do not reopen affected areas until cleared.
