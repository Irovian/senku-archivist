# Earthquake

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} protects residents, staff, visitors, and operations during and after an earthquake.

## During Shaking
Staff should protect themselves first so they can assist residents after shaking stops. When possible, staff should move away from windows, exterior walls, unsecured furniture, and falling hazards. Staff should not run through the building during active shaking.

Residents should be protected in place when movement would create more risk. Staff may use available furniture or positioning to reduce injury risk when it can be done safely.

## Initial Assessment
After shaking stops and it is safe to move, departments should assess residents and staff for injuries, check immediate hazards, and report findings to the command center. Maintenance or assigned staff should assess structure, utilities, fire protection, elevators, medical gases, water, power, and other building systems.

The command center should activate incident command as needed, prepare for aftershocks, determine whether relocation or evacuation is required, and coordinate with emergency responders or the local emergency operations center.

## Resident Care and Operations
Nursing should prioritize injured residents, residents dependent on power or oxygen, and residents in damaged areas. Departments should continue essential services only in areas that are safe to occupy.

## Recovery
Recovery includes continued aftershock awareness, damage documentation, utility and system inspection, resident and family communication, cost tracking, staff support, and after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Protect yourself during shaking so you can help residents afterward.
- Do not use elevators until cleared.
- Report injuries, damage, utility concerns, and unsafe areas quickly.
- Expect aftershocks and keep residents away from hazards.
- Evacuation or relocation decisions come from leadership and responder guidance.
