# Loss of Electric Service

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when normal electric service is interrupted, unstable, partially unavailable, or expected to be unavailable long enough to affect resident care, life safety, building operations, or essential services.

## Recognition and Activation
Staff should report loss of power, partial power loss, abnormal electrical conditions, equipment shutdowns, emergency power transfer problems, lighting loss, elevator impacts, call-system impacts, or other power-dependent service problems immediately.

If the emergency operations plan is active, the command center or assigned leader should coordinate the response under {{emergency_code_alert}} or the facility's assigned emergency activation language. The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the outage requires emergency power operation, load reduction, resident relocation, census reduction, evacuation, regulatory notification, fire watch, vendor support, or activation of related procedures.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by the event, duration, resident-care impact, life-safety impact, or facility policy.

## Electric Service Assessment
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess the outage source, affected areas, utility status, emergency power transfer, electrical panels, elevators, fire and life safety systems, communication systems, security systems, medication storage, kitchen equipment, laundry, HVAC, clinical equipment, and other power-dependent services.

The electric utility or outage contact is {{electric_utility_contact}}. Emergency power outlets, panels, circuits, or receptacles are identified by {{emergency_power_circuit_identification}}. Critical power-dependent equipment or systems requiring priority assessment include {{critical_power_dependent_equipment}}.

Services normally supported by emergency power include {{emergency_power_supported_services}}. Services that cannot be provided or may be limited while on emergency power include {{emergency_power_unsupported_services}}. Areas and critical equipment supported by emergency power include {{emergency_power_supported_areas_equipment}}.

## Resident Care and Load Priorities
The command center or assigned leader should prioritize available power for life safety, resident care, clinical support, medication security, emergency lighting, fire and life safety systems, communications support, food and water support, and other essential operations.

Departments should identify residents whose care depends on powered equipment, powered environmental support, electronic medication systems, elevators, call systems, or other power-dependent services. Staff should implement battery backup, manual processes, relocation, transfer, additional monitoring, or alternate care measures as directed.

Nonessential equipment should be turned off or disconnected when needed to preserve emergency power capacity or protect critical equipment. Staff should not connect extension cords, generators, electrical equipment, or temporary power sources unless trained and authorized to do so.

## Department Coordination
Departments should report service impacts, equipment failures, temperature concerns, elevator entrapment concerns, food safety concerns, medication storage concerns, resident-care limitations, and staffing needs to the command center or assigned leader.

If the outage affects generator operation, the facility should activate the Loss of Emergency Generator Power procedure. If the outage affects water, sewer, heating, cooling, fire protection, communications, or other services, the related utility procedure should also be considered.

## Recovery
After normal electric service is restored, maintenance should verify safe operation of electrical systems, emergency power equipment, elevators when applicable, critical equipment, alarms, life-safety systems, medication refrigerators, and other affected building systems.

Departments should report remaining service problems, spoiled supplies, equipment damage, resident-care impacts, emergency purchases, vendor actions, and documentation needed for recovery.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report electric service problems immediately.
- Emergency power may support only selected services, areas, or equipment.
- Protect residents who depend on powered equipment or powered environmental support first.
- Turn off or disconnect nonessential loads only as directed.
- Generator failure, dialysis electric-service needs, and detailed equipment/fuel tables belong in their dedicated procedures or resource lists.
