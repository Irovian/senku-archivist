# Loss of Emergency Generator Power

## Purpose
This procedure describes the response when emergency generator power fails, is unavailable, or cannot safely support required facility operations during a power outage.

## Activation and Initial Actions
When emergency generator power is lost or unreliable, staff should notify the command center or assigned leader immediately. If the emergency operations plan is active, the response should be coordinated under {{emergency_code_alert}} or the facility's assigned emergency activation language.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether residents, staff, or essential systems are in immediate danger and whether evacuation, relocation, census reduction, or shelter-in-place modifications are required. Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by law, outage duration, life safety impact, or facility policy.

## Life Safety and Resident Care Priorities
The first operational priority is to protect residents who depend on powered equipment, environmental controls, elevators, call systems, clinical equipment, medication systems, or other essential services. Staff should identify residents whose care may be affected by loss of emergency power and implement alternate care measures, relocation, transfer, manual processes, or additional monitoring as directed.

Services normally supported by emergency power include {{emergency_power_supported_services}}. Services that cannot be provided or may be limited while on emergency power include {{emergency_power_unsupported_services}}. Areas and critical equipment served by emergency power include {{emergency_power_supported_areas_equipment}}.

## Maintenance and Power-System Response
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess generator status, fuel supply, transfer switches, electrical distribution, connected loads, alarms, equipment damage, and any unsafe conditions. The generator fuel type is {{generator_fuel_type}}, with an estimated full-load runtime of {{generator_full_load_runtime}} before refueling. If safe load shedding is available, the estimated extended runtime is {{generator_load_shed_runtime}}.

Maintenance actions should follow facility-approved procedures and manufacturer or vendor guidance. Staff should not connect, disconnect, reset, refuel, or bypass generator or electrical equipment unless trained and authorized to do so. If a portable or backup generator is used, connection and back-feed prevention steps must be validated by qualified maintenance, vendor, or electrical personnel.

## Operations Under Reduced or No Emergency Power
The command center or assigned leader should prioritize available power for life safety, resident care, clinical support, medication security, emergency lighting, fire and life safety systems, communications support, food and water support, and other essential operations. Nonessential loads may be shut down to extend runtime or protect critical equipment.

Departments should report service impacts, equipment failures, temperature concerns, elevator entrapment concerns, food safety concerns, resident-care limitations, and staffing needs. Dining, nursing, maintenance, and other departments should coordinate alternate workflows when normal power-dependent systems are unavailable.

## Recovery
After normal or stable emergency power is restored, maintenance should verify safe operation of electrical systems, generator equipment, elevators when applicable, critical equipment, alarms, and affected building systems. Departments should report remaining service problems, spoiled supplies, equipment damage, resident-care impacts, emergency purchases, and documentation needed for recovery.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report emergency generator problems immediately.
- Protect residents who depend on powered equipment or powered environmental support first.
- Emergency power may support only selected areas, services, or equipment.
- Only trained and authorized personnel should operate or connect generator and electrical equipment.
- Recovery includes verifying systems after power returns, not only restoring power.
