# Loss of Air Conditioning / High Heat

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when air conditioning is unavailable, insufficient, or when indoor or outdoor heat conditions may threaten resident, staff, visitor, or building safety.

## Recognition and Activation
High heat or loss of air conditioning may result from HVAC failure, electric service loss, generator limitations, extreme weather, equipment damage, water intrusion, or other utility disruption. Staff should report unsafe temperatures, air conditioning failure, resident heat stress, staff heat stress, equipment heat concerns, or inability to cool occupied areas immediately.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the event requires cooling areas, portable cooling, hydration support, resident relocation, staff work modification, regulatory notification, evacuation, or activation of related electric, generator, water, cooking, or recovery procedures.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by the event, duration, temperature, heat-related illness, death, resident impact, evacuation risk, or facility policy. High heat reporting contacts include {{high_heat_reporting_contacts}}.

## Cooling and Temperature Assessment
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess HVAC status, expected outage duration, affected areas, outdoor conditions, indoor temperatures, electrical or generator support, portable cooling options, ventilation, and sensitive equipment impacts.

Temperature monitoring should include {{temperature_monitoring_locations}}. The maximum safe temperature or heat-index escalation threshold is {{maximum_safe_temperature_threshold}}.

Cooling areas or common areas that may be used during high heat include {{cooling_area_locations}}. Portable cooling equipment, vendors, or rental sources include {{portable_cooling_sources}}.

## Resident Protection
Departments should close blinds or curtains in direct sun, reduce heat-producing activities, avoid unnecessary resident exertion, encourage hydration as clinically appropriate, monitor vulnerable residents, and move residents away from overheated areas when directed.

Nursing and clinical leaders should identify residents at higher risk from heat, including residents with limited mobility, dehydration risk, cardiac or respiratory vulnerability, cognitive impairment, fever, medication-related heat sensitivity, or dependence on power-supported environmental controls.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether residents can be consolidated into cooled areas, whether staff should rotate out of high-heat areas, and whether relocation, transfer, census reduction, or evacuation is needed.

## Department Coordination
Maintenance should coordinate HVAC repair, portable cooling, generator or power interface, and equipment protection. Dining and nursing should coordinate hydration, meal modifications, and resident monitoring. Other departments should report temperature conditions, resident discomfort, staff heat stress, supply needs, and service limitations.

If high heat or air conditioning loss is caused by electric service loss or generator failure, the related power procedure should also be activated.

## Recovery
Before normal operations resume, maintenance should confirm stable cooling, safe equipment operation, and acceptable temperatures in occupied areas. Departments should continue monitoring residents and temperatures until conditions remain stable.

Remaining resident-care impacts, heat-related illness reports, equipment damage, vendor actions, emergency purchases, relocation decisions, and lessons learned should be documented for recovery.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report unsafe heat or air conditioning failure immediately.
- High heat can become an emergency quickly for vulnerable residents.
- Cooling areas, hydration, resident monitoring, and relocation may all be needed.
- Portable cooling should be coordinated through leadership and maintenance.
- Electric/generator response and water supply details remain in their dedicated procedures.
