# Loss of Water Service During Dialysis

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} protects residents receiving dialysis or dependent on dialysis-related services when water service, water pressure, water quality, or dialysis water treatment support is interrupted, contaminated, restricted, or unavailable.

## Recognition and Activation
Staff should report water loss, low water pressure, boil-water notice, water contamination concern, water treatment alarm, reverse-osmosis or dialysis water equipment problem, inability to support dialysis treatment, or any water condition affecting dialysis immediately.

Dialysis staff, contracted dialysis providers, or dialysis clinical support should be contacted through {{dialysis_staff_contact_process}}. Clinical staff should follow dialysis provider direction for treatment interruption, treatment delay, water quality restrictions, resident monitoring, transfer, or emergency medical services.

The Loss of Water Service / Contamination of Water Supply procedure should also be activated when the disruption affects building water, potable water, sanitation, food service, laundry, cooling, fire protection, or other water-dependent services.

## Dialysis Safety Priorities
The command center or assigned clinical leader should identify residents receiving dialysis, residents scheduled for dialysis, residents who may miss treatment, and residents whose condition may worsen if treatment is delayed.

Clinical staff should assess treatment status, access safety, fluid status, medication timing, diet/fluid restrictions, transportation needs, and whether alternate dialysis arrangements are required.

The dialysis water contingency process is {{dialysis_water_contingency_process}}. Transfer, alternate treatment, or receiving-provider arrangements are described by {{dialysis_resident_transfer_arrangements}}.

## Water Supply and Treatment Assessment
Potable water sources available to the facility include {{potable_water_sources}}. Potable water vendors or alternate sources include {{potable_water_vendors}}.

Maintenance, dialysis staff, and the command center or assigned leader should determine whether available water is safe and adequate for dialysis support. Dialysis should not continue or restart until the dialysis provider or qualified staff confirm that water supply, pressure, quality, treatment, and equipment conditions are safe.

If water is contaminated, under restriction, or of uncertain quality, the facility should assume dialysis water is not safe until the dialysis provider or appropriate authority confirms otherwise.

## Operating During Water Loss
If dialysis cannot continue safely, clinical staff should stop or modify treatment according to dialysis provider direction, monitor residents, arrange transfer or alternate dialysis, and document missed or interrupted treatment.

The command center or assigned leader should coordinate transportation, receiving-provider communication, resident and representative updates, diet/fluid restriction communication, medication coordination, and continuity of clinical documentation.

If water loss affects electric service, generator function, sewer, oxygen/vacuum, fire protection, heating, cooling, or food service, the related procedure should also be considered.

## Recovery
Before dialysis services resume, dialysis staff or the contracted provider should confirm that water supply is stable, water quality is acceptable, required treatment equipment has been checked, alarms are cleared, and residents can be treated safely.

Departments should document resident impacts, interrupted treatments, transfers, missed treatments, water quality issues, vendor actions, regulatory notices, and follow-up needed with dialysis providers.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Water loss or water contamination during dialysis is a clinical escalation event.
- Dialysis staff or the contracted dialysis provider should direct treatment interruption and restart decisions.
- Potable water availability does not automatically mean dialysis water is safe.
- Resident transfer or alternate dialysis arrangements may be needed quickly.
- Building water-service procedures remain active companions to this procedure.
