# Building Search Checklist

## Purpose
This checklist supports command-center tracking when departments are assigned to search areas during a bomb threat, suspicious package, missing resident, or other incident requiring a structured building search.

## Use of Checklist
The command center should assign search areas, search priority, staff responsible, start and finish times, and reporting expectations. Staff should search only when directed by leadership and only when conditions are safe.

Search teams should report whether each assigned area is clear, not clear, inaccessible, or requires responder follow-up. Suspicious items should not be touched or moved. Findings should be reported immediately through the assigned communication channel.

## Search Record Fields
The search record should capture: incident name or type, date and time, unit or department, specific area searched, search priority, assigned staff, clear or not clear status, finish time, findings, inaccessible areas, escalation made, and the command-center person receiving the report.

## Follow-Up
The command center should reconcile all assigned areas, identify gaps, escalate unresolved areas, retain the checklist with incident documentation, and include search process issues in after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Search only when assigned and when it is safe.
- Report suspicious items without touching or moving them.
- Use clear, consistent status language for each assigned area.
- Unsearched or inaccessible areas must be escalated.
- The completed checklist becomes part of incident documentation.
