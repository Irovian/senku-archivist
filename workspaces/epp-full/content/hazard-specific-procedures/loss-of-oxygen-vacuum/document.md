# Loss of Oxygen / Vacuum

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when piped oxygen, medical vacuum, medical air, portable oxygen supply, suction capability, or related medical gas support is interrupted, unstable, unavailable, or unsafe for resident care.

## Recognition and Activation
Staff should report oxygen system alarms, loss of oxygen pressure, vacuum failure, suction failure, medical gas odor, damaged outlets, equipment malfunction, low portable oxygen supply, or inability to support prescribed oxygen or suction needs immediately.

The command center or assigned clinical leader should determine whether the event requires respiratory therapy support, portable oxygen deployment, portable suction deployment, emergency medical services, resident relocation, transfer, vendor repair, regulatory notification, evacuation, or activation of related electric, generator, fire, or utility procedures.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by resident impact, duration, clinical risk, system impairment, evacuation, or facility policy.

## Clinical Safety Priorities
Nursing should immediately identify residents using oxygen, suction, ventilator support, CPAP/BiPAP, tracheostomy care, or other respiratory support affected by the disruption.

Respiratory therapy or clinical respiratory support should be contacted through {{respiratory_therapy_contact_process}}. Staff should follow clinical direction for portable oxygen, portable suction, manual support, monitoring frequency, emergency medical services, relocation, or transfer.

Staff should prioritize residents with life-sustaining oxygen or suction needs and should not operate medical gas valves, emergency shutoffs, or quick-connect equipment unless trained and authorized.

## System and Supply Assessment
The medical gas system is described as {{medical_gas_system_description}}. {{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess alarms, source supply, zone valves, outlets, manifold or bulk supply status, emergency shutoff status, power dependency, vendor repair needs, and affected resident-care areas.

The medical gas or respiratory equipment supplier contact is {{medical_gas_supplier_contact}}. Portable oxygen supply is located or obtained through {{portable_oxygen_supply_location}}. Oxygen shutoff locations or references include {{oxygen_shutoff_locations}}.

Emergency oxygen quick-connect or low-pressure gaseous oxygen connection information is {{oxygen_quick_connect_location}}. Portable suction equipment is located or obtained through {{portable_suction_location}}.

## Operating Under Oxygen or Vacuum Loss
The command center or assigned clinical leader should coordinate resident monitoring, portable supply use, equipment conservation, staff assignments, vendor response, and transfer decisions. Departments should report additional residents whose respiratory support needs change during the event.

If the loss is caused by electric service failure, generator failure, fire protection impairment, evacuation, or another utility disruption, the related procedure should also be activated.

Sewer, waste, and force-flushing instructions belong in the sewer/waste procedure and should not be managed under this oxygen/vacuum procedure unless the same incident also affects sanitation.

## Recovery
Before normal oxygen or vacuum service resumes, maintenance and clinical leadership should confirm that affected systems are operating safely, alarms are resolved, vendor or respiratory support has cleared the system for use, and residents have been reassessed.

Departments should document resident impacts, portable oxygen or suction use, vendor actions, system repairs, transfers, supply depletion, regulatory notifications, and any follow-up actions needed.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report oxygen, suction, vacuum, or medical gas concerns immediately.
- Resident respiratory support needs take priority over equipment troubleshooting.
- Portable oxygen and portable suction locations must be known before an outage.
- Only trained staff should operate medical gas shutoffs or emergency connection points.
- Sewer/waste content belongs in the sewer/waste procedure unless the same event affects both systems.
