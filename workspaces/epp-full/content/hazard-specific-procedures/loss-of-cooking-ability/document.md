# Loss of Cooking Ability

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} maintains safe meal service when normal cooking equipment, kitchen ventilation, fuel, power, or other food-preparation capability is unavailable.

## Recognition and Activation
Loss of cooking ability may result from electric service loss, gas or propane service loss, steam loss, water loss, kitchen equipment failure, kitchen hood suppression impairment, fire damage, flood damage, or other conditions that make normal cooking unsafe or impossible.

Staff should report cooking equipment failure, kitchen ventilation or hood suppression problems, fuel or power loss, unsafe kitchen conditions, emergency menu needs, or food-service disruption immediately.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the event requires non-cooking menus, alternate cooking methods, meal-service modification, emergency food supply access, vendor support, regulatory notification, relocation of food preparation, or activation of related electric, gas, steam, water, or fire protection procedures.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by the event, duration, resident impact, food safety risk, or facility policy.

## Food Service Assessment
{{dining_resource_owner_title}} should assess available staff, safe kitchen access, remaining equipment, emergency food supply, special diets, food temperatures, water availability, disposable supplies, fuel or power dependency, and the ability to provide meals near normal service times.

Emergency food supplies are located at {{emergency_food_supply_location}}. Emergency non-cooking menus are located at {{non_cooking_menu_location}}. Authorized kitchen or emergency food access is available through {{kitchen_access_method}}.

Alternate cooking, warming, meal preparation, or meal sourcing methods include {{alternate_cooking_methods}}.

## Meal-Service Priorities
Food service should prioritize resident meals, special diets, hydration support, medically necessary nutrition, and safe food handling. Staff and visitor meal support may be modified when supplies, staffing, or preparation capability are limited.

Dining services should coordinate with nursing to identify residents with special dietary needs, swallowing precautions, diabetic menus, texture-modified diets, supplements, or other nutrition needs that may be affected by emergency menus or altered food preparation.

If normal cooking is unsafe because of kitchen hood suppression impairment, gas odor, power instability, water contamination, or equipment damage, staff should not use the affected equipment until cleared by authorized personnel.

## Department Coordination
Departments should report available food supplies, resident nutrition concerns, food safety issues, disposable supply needs, meal delivery barriers, and staffing constraints to the command center or assigned leader.

If the cooking disruption is caused by electric service loss, generator failure, gas or propane loss, steam pressure loss, water loss, sewer/waste disruption, or fire protection system impairment, the related procedure should also be activated.

## Recovery
Before normal cooking resumes, responsible staff should confirm that equipment, utilities, ventilation, fire suppression, water service, and food safety conditions are safe. Dining services should restock emergency food supplies, document emergency menus used, report food loss or emergency purchases, and identify any resident nutrition impacts.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report loss of cooking ability or unsafe kitchen conditions immediately.
- Non-cooking menus and emergency food supplies protect meal continuity when cooking is unavailable.
- Special diets and hydration needs still require active coordination.
- Do not use cooking equipment affected by unsafe power, fuel, water, ventilation, or fire suppression conditions.
- Detailed emergency food lists and menu worksheets belong in resource/list artifacts, not this procedure.
