
# Department-Specific Evacuation Guidelines

## Purpose
These guidelines describe how {{facility_name}} departments support evacuation, relocation, or sheltering decisions during a hazard-specific response.

## General Expectations
Each department should understand its role in resident movement, life safety, essential services, communication, documentation, and recovery. Department leaders should maintain staff awareness of evacuation routes, holding areas, resident support needs, equipment needs, and reporting expectations.

When evacuation or relocation is ordered, departments should follow the facility evacuation plan and incident command direction. No department should independently move residents outside assigned relocation or evacuation priorities unless immediate life safety requires it.

## Resident Care Departments
Nursing and clinical staff should identify residents who need oxygen, mobility assistance, supervision, medications, medical records, treatments, dialysis coordination, ventilator support, or other special handling. Resident tracking, clinical documentation, and handoff information should be maintained throughout movement.

## Support Departments
Maintenance, housekeeping, laundry, food service, activities, social services, therapy, transportation, administration, and other departments should support routes, equipment, supplies, resident comfort, records, communication, staffing, and receiving-area setup as assigned.

## Communication and Documentation
Departments should report completed assignments, unmet needs, resident movement issues, staffing concerns, supply gaps, and safety hazards to the command center or assigned leader. Documentation should support resident tracking, regulatory reporting, cost tracking, and after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Follow incident command and the evacuation plan.
- Keep resident tracking current.
- Report hazards, delays, and unmet needs quickly.
- Preserve essential medications, records, equipment, and resident dignity.
- Do not freelance movement decisions outside immediate life-safety needs.
