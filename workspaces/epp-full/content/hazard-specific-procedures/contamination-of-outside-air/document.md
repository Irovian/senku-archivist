# Contamination of Outside Air

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when outside air contamination, smoke, chemical release, odor, or airborne hazard may affect indoor air quality.

## Activation
The command center or person in charge should activate this procedure when staff identify unsafe outside air, authorities issue shelter-in-place or air-quality instructions, a nearby release is reported, or residents and staff may be exposed through ventilation or open doors and windows.

## Immediate Protective Actions
Staff should close exterior doors and windows when directed, limit traffic through affected entrances, and move residents away from areas with odor, smoke, visible contamination, or irritation. Maintenance or assigned staff should evaluate air-handling systems and adjust outside-air intake when safe and appropriate.

The command center should contact emergency responders, public health, emergency management, or utility partners when the source or health risk is unclear.

## Resident Care and Operations
Nursing should monitor residents for breathing difficulty, eye or throat irritation, dizziness, nausea, or other symptoms. Residents who use oxygen, have respiratory conditions, or are medically fragile should be prioritized for monitoring and relocation if needed.

Departments should continue essential services while reducing unnecessary movement and preserving indoor air protection.

## Recovery
Normal ventilation, access, and activities should resume only when the command center or authorities determine the hazard has cleared. Recovery includes documenting symptoms, actions taken, system changes, responder guidance, and improvement items.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Close or avoid exterior openings when directed.
- Report odors, smoke, visible contamination, or symptoms immediately.
- Maintenance coordinates ventilation changes.
- Monitor respiratory-risk residents closely.
- Resume normal operations only after clearance.
