# Loss of Fire Protection Systems

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} responds when fire alarm, detection, sprinkler, suppression, monitoring, or related fire protection systems are impaired, unavailable, or unreliable.

## Recognition and Activation
Fire protection system impairment may involve fire alarm failure, smoke detector failure, sprinkler or water-flow impairment, valve tamper conditions, kitchen hood suppression impairment, alarm monitoring problems, power-related system failure, or other conditions that reduce fire detection, notification, or suppression capability.

Staff should report fire protection system trouble conditions, alarms, supervisory signals, visible damage, water-flow concerns, suppression system outage, or other fire protection problems immediately.

The command center or assigned leader should determine whether the event requires fire department notification, fire watch, system isolation, repair vendor support, regulatory notification, cooking restrictions, resident relocation, evacuation readiness, or activation of related electric, water, cooking, or fire procedures.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by the event, duration, fire protection impact, resident impact, fire watch requirement, or facility policy.

## Fire Protection System Assessment
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess the affected system, expected impairment duration, cause, affected building areas, fire alarm panel status, sprinkler or suppression system status, water supply impact, alarm monitoring status, and repair or vendor needs.

The facility fire protection systems are described as {{fire_protection_system_description}}. The fire situation code word or alert language is {{code_word_term_identify_fire_situation}}. Fire department contact or reporting instructions are {{fire_department_contact}}.

Additional impairment notifications may include {{fire_impairment_notification_list}}.

## Fire Watch and Interim Controls
The command center or assigned leader should determine whether a fire watch is required. Fire watch procedures, checklists, logs, or documentation instructions are located at {{fire_watch_procedure_location}}.

Interim controls may include increased rounds, staff briefings, posting affected areas, removing ignition hazards, restricting hot work, restricting cooking under impaired hood suppression, verifying exits remain clear, and increasing awareness of manual fire reporting methods.

If the kitchen hood suppression system is impaired, cooking equipment under the hood should not be used until cleared by authorized personnel.

## Resident Care and Department Coordination
Departments should understand which fire protection systems are impaired, how to report smoke or fire, how to alert others if the alarm system is not reliable, and whether evacuation readiness or relocation is needed.

Maintenance, leadership, and department heads should document impairment start time, notifications, fire watch rounds, vendor actions, corrective action, staff instructions, and restoration time.

## Recovery
Normal operations may resume only after the affected fire protection system is repaired, tested, restored, and accepted by authorized personnel or the authority having jurisdiction when required.

Fire watch or interim controls should remain in place until the command center or assigned leader confirms they may stop. Remaining documentation, vendor reports, costs, corrective actions, and lessons learned should be retained for recovery and compliance review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Report fire protection system problems immediately.
- Fire watch may be required when detection, alarm, sprinkler, or suppression systems are impaired.
- Staff need to know how to report fire manually if the alarm system is not reliable.
- Cooking under an impaired kitchen hood suppression system should stop until cleared.
- Fire response procedures and fire watch forms remain separate artifacts.
