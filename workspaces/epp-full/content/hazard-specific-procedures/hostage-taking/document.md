
# Hostage Taking

## Purpose
This procedure describes how {{facility_name}} protects residents, staff, visitors, and operations during a hostage situation or suspected hostage situation.

## Immediate Actions
Staff who become aware of a hostage situation should move away from immediate danger when safe, avoid confrontation, warn others discreetly, and call 911 or activate the emergency notification process as soon as it can be done safely. Staff should not attempt to negotiate, disarm, restrain, or physically intervene unless there is no safer option to protect life.

If safe, staff should note the location, number of persons involved, descriptions, weapons observed, injuries, and any known access routes. This information should be provided to law enforcement and incident command.

## Incident Command and Security
Leadership should activate incident command as needed, restrict access to the affected area, preserve safe routes for residents and staff, and coordinate with law enforcement. Staff should follow law enforcement direction once responders assume control of the scene.

Departments should account for residents, staff, and visitors in or near the affected area. Nursing should identify residents who may need urgent clinical support if relocation, sheltering, or delayed services occur.

## Communication
Only assigned leadership should communicate with law enforcement, families, representatives, media, or outside agencies. Staff should not release information, speculation, or resident details outside approved communication channels.

## Recovery
Recovery includes accounting for all persons, providing medical and emotional support, preserving evidence when directed, documenting decisions and actions, restoring affected operations, and completing after-action review.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Get away from danger if it is safe to do so.
- Do not confront or negotiate with a hostage taker.
- Call for law enforcement and report clear facts.
- Keep residents away from the affected area.
- Follow leadership and law enforcement direction.
