# Fire Watch Checklist

## Purpose
This checklist supports {{facility_name}} when a fire alarm, sprinkler, detection, suppression, monitoring, water supply, or other fire protection component is impaired and the facility must conduct fire watch until the system is restored or the authority having jurisdiction provides other direction.

## Activation Criteria
Fire watch should be considered when the fire protection system described as {{fire_protection_system_description}} is impaired, taken out of service, unreliable, not monitored, affected by water loss, affected by electric service loss, or otherwise unable to provide expected protection.

The command center or assigned leader should coordinate with {{maintenance_resource_owner_title}}, the fire department or fire marshal, alarm monitoring company, administrator, and other required parties.

Fire department contact or reporting instructions are {{fire_department_contact}}. The fire impairment notification list is {{fire_impairment_notification_list}}. Facility fire watch forms, procedures, or documentation are located at {{fire_watch_procedure_location}}.

## Initial Fire Watch Setup
- Confirm the affected building, unit, system, zone, or equipment.
- Notify required internal leaders and external authorities.
- Determine the required fire watch scope, patrol route, staffing, and documentation method.
- Assign trained staff to fire watch duties.
- Establish the fire watch round interval as {{fire_watch_round_interval}} or as directed by the authority having jurisdiction.
- Confirm staff have communication equipment and a way to report fire, smoke, odor, alarm, sprinkler, or life-safety concerns immediately.
- Remind staff that fire watch does not replace evacuation, fire response, or emergency notification duties.

## Fire Watch Rounds
Assigned staff should patrol affected areas at the required interval and look for smoke, fire, burning odor, blocked exits, unsafe storage, impaired egress, alarm trouble, sprinkler leakage, heat sources, electrical hazards, oxygen or medical gas concerns, and other fire or life-safety risks.

Each round should document:
- Date and time of round.
- Area, unit, floor, zone, or route checked.
- Staff member completing the round.
- Conditions observed.
- Deficiencies or hazards found.
- Corrective action, escalation, or notification completed.

Any sign of fire, smoke, burning odor, alarm activation, sprinkler activation, resident danger, or blocked evacuation route should be escalated immediately using the facility fire response procedure.

## Ongoing Coordination
The command center or assigned leader should monitor staffing, resident safety, system repair status, authority notifications, documentation completeness, and whether additional evacuation or relocation precautions are needed.

Maintenance should coordinate repair, vendor response, impairment tagging, restoration testing, and communication with the alarm monitoring company or fire protection vendor when applicable.

If the impairment is caused by electric service loss, generator failure, water loss, sprinkler impairment, fire alarm failure, severe weather, construction, or another hazard, the related procedure should also be activated.

## Termination and Recovery
Fire watch should continue until the affected system is restored, tested, accepted for service, and released from fire watch by the command center or assigned leader in accordance with authority direction and facility policy.

Before terminating fire watch, confirm that required notifications have been updated, documentation is complete, hazards found during rounds have been addressed, and staff know normal fire protection monitoring has resumed.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Fire watch is an active patrol and documentation process during fire protection impairment.
- Staff must escalate smoke, fire, burning odor, alarm, egress, or life-safety concerns immediately.
- The assigned round interval must be followed until fire watch is terminated.
- Fire watch documentation must be complete enough to show who checked which areas and when.
- The Loss of Fire Protection Systems procedure remains the umbrella procedure for impairment response.
