# Natural Gas / Propane Odor / Leak

## Purpose
This procedure describes immediate staff response when natural gas, propane, or another gas fuel odor or leak is suspected at {{facility_name}}.

## Recognition and Immediate Safety Actions
Any gas or propane odor, suspected leak, hissing sound near gas equipment, damaged gas line, unexplained pilot-light outage, or utility notice of a gas hazard should be treated as a potential life-safety event.

Staff in or near the affected area should move residents, visitors, and staff away from the odor or suspected leak when it is safe to do so. Staff should notify the command center, supervisor, or assigned leader immediately. If maintenance or leadership is not available, staff should contact emergency services according to facility policy.

Staff should avoid actions that could create ignition. This includes open flames, smoking, operating electrical switches in the affected area, using elevators in or near the affected area, restarting equipment, or attempting to locate the leak without authorization.

## Incident Command and Notifications
The command center or assigned leader should determine whether to evacuate the affected area, restrict access, activate building lockdown or traffic control, notify emergency services, notify the gas utility or propane supplier, shut down air-handling equipment, or activate additional evacuation or sheltering procedures.

The facility gas service type is {{gas_service_type}}. The gas utility, propane supplier, or emergency service contact is {{gas_utility_contact}}. Gas shut-off locations or the related utility shut-off reference are {{gas_shutoff_locations}}.

Notification to {{regulatory_agency_name}} or another authority should occur when required by the event, resident impact, life-safety impact, evacuation, service interruption, or facility policy.

## Maintenance and Utility Controls
{{maintenance_resource_owner_title}} should assess the situation only when it is safe and within the role's training and authority. If the source cannot be immediately confirmed as safe, or if a leak is suspected, emergency services and the gas utility or propane supplier should be contacted.

Gas supply should be shut off only by trained and authorized personnel or by emergency responders, the utility, or the vendor when required. Staff should not relight pilot lights, restart gas appliances, or restore gas service until the area and equipment have been cleared by authorized personnel.

## Resident Care and Area Control
Nursing and department leaders should account for residents, staff, and visitors moved from the affected area. Residents who require relocation, transfer, additional monitoring, warmth, oxygen support, or other care modifications should be prioritized.

The command center or assigned leader should document affected areas, notifications, resident movement, utility or responder instructions, shut-off actions, service limitations, and recovery steps.

## Recovery
Normal operations may resume only after the source of the odor or leak has been addressed, the area has been cleared for occupancy, equipment has been checked, and departments understand which services may restart.

If gas or propane service remains unavailable after the immediate hazard is controlled, the Loss of Natural Gas / Propane procedure should be used for continuity actions.

## What Staff Should Understand
- Treat gas or propane odor as a potential life-safety event.
- Move people away from the affected area when safe.
- Avoid flames, smoking, electrical switches, elevators near the affected area, and unauthorized equipment operation.
- Only trained and authorized personnel or responders should shut off or restore gas service.
- Prolonged gas-service loss belongs in the Loss of Natural Gas / Propane procedure.
