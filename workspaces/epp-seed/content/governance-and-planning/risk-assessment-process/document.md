# Risk Assessment Process

## Purpose
This document explains how {{facility_name}} identifies emergency risks, prioritizes them, and turns those findings into planning and mitigation work.

## Process Overview
{{facility_name}} uses a documented risk-assessment process that combines facility-based hazards with community-based hazards. The process follows an all-hazards approach and should account for events that could disrupt resident care, staffing, utilities, communications, security, supply availability, or access to the building.

The assessment should reflect the facility's actual care level and operating designation, including its current role as a {{facility_type_designation}} and any resident population characteristics that affect emergency planning.

The main output of this process is a current hazard vulnerability assessment. The assessment should help the facility decide which risks need the most attention and which strategies must be built into emergency plans, procedures, resources, and training.

The risk-assessment process should not be treated as a one-time exercise. It should be reviewed at least annually and whenever the facility experiences a major operational change, a serious event, or new information from the community that changes the facility's risk picture.

Community coordination matters here because the facility's risks are not limited to what happens inside the building. The assessment should be informed by relevant partners, local emergency management information, and other credible planning inputs that affect how the facility prepares for local hazards.

## Core Expectations
- Identify the hazards most likely to affect resident safety and facility operations.
- Consider both facility-specific conditions and community-wide threats.
- Document the method used to rank or prioritize risks.
- Turn identified risks into planning, mitigation, and response strategies.
- Keep evidence that the facility reviews the assessment and updates it when conditions change.
